# The customer pitch (the UI must make every line true and visible)

Autonomy: proof of what your marketing really earns, before you trust it with sending

The problem you already know: you send the same "10% off" email to everyone who hasn't bought in 60 days. Some of them would have bought anyway, so you just gave away margin. Some ignore you. Some get annoyed and unsubscribe. And at the end of the month, nobody can tell you whether the campaign actually made money.

What Autonomy does first: it measures. A small random group of your customers is never contacted, and everyone else is compared with them. You see the extra profit the messages really caused, with an honest range, or "not sure yet" when the range includes zero. Only then does it ask for more control: its decisions come with reasons, and it starts by deciding without sending anything.

What it does for each customer: it looks after every customer one by one, every day, and asks one question: "What's the most profitable thing to do for this person right now, including doing nothing?" Then it acts inside the limits you set, watches what happens, and shows you the money it made.

How it works for you: five simple screens
1. Connect. Upload your customer and order files, or connect your database. Our Data Doctor cleans up messy data and asks before changing anything.
2. Goal. Type what you want, in plain words: "Win back customers who stopped buying, $5,000 this month." Or pick one of the opportunities it found for you: "1,900 customers are drifting away. Want me to handle them?"
3. Plan. It shows you its plan in plain English: who it will contact, which offers it may use, which channels, at most how often, quiet hours and budget. It also shows you the actual messages. You approve once.
4. Live. A daily briefing: "Yesterday I contacted 341 customers, left 1,200 alone, and here's why." Tap any customer and see every decision with a reason: "Sent Priya a reminder with no discount: she always buys at full price, and email is cheapest."
5. Proof. How much extra profit it made compared with a group it never contacted, with an honest range. If it isn't sure yet, it says so. It also compares itself with a simple rule anyone could set up, so you can see whether the AI earns its keep.

What makes it different
- It proves every rupee, or dollar. A small random group of customers is never contacted. Autonomy compares everyone else against them, so you see real extra profit, not "attributed revenue". It also measures itself against a simple rule anyone could set up. It does not claim a return multiple: the profit shown is already after the costs it could charge.
- It doesn't waste your discounts. Most tools target people "likely to buy". Autonomy targets people whose behaviour it can actually change. Loyal buyers get a reminder, not a coupon. Price-sensitive shoppers get an offer only when it pays for itself.
- It knows when to stay quiet. Every message has a cost: annoyance, unsubscribes, spam complaints. Autonomy weighs that cost every time, so "do nothing" often wins. Less spam, healthier lists.
- It chooses per person. Who, when, which channel, which offer, which message, and how often. Channels: email today; WhatsApp is next (the app simulates it, and your own WhatsApp account cannot be connected yet); SMS and your own tools through a hand-off. Timing: it learns when each person actually reads their messages.
- You stay in control. Shadow: it decides and shows you, but sends nothing. Approve: you approve each batch with one click. Autopilot: it acts on its own inside the limits you set; the Proof page says "ready for Autopilot" only when even the low end of the profit range is above zero. Hard rules it can never break: consent, quiet hours, weekly limits, budget, no promotions after a complaint. It auto-pauses itself if complaints or unsubscribes spike. One big Pause button.
- Messages are checked before they can go out. They come from approved templates in your voice. A strict editor checks every one (brand voice, no false claims, no made-up numbers, unsubscribe line present, channel rules), and the server refuses to approve a message the editor did not pass.
- It knows your products. It predicts when each customer is due to reorder, and suggests complementary products. This works best where customers buy related things. On one public shop's order history its suggestions got about 58% more purchases than recommending best-sellers (34.7% against 22.0%). That was the best case: on six other public order histories the gain was smaller (+8.8, +4.6 and +1.2 points on three of them), could not be measured on one, and was negative on two (-1.3 and -2.1 points on Ta-Feng and Superstore). Where best-sellers do better, these suggestions do worse. The full table is in [REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md](REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md).

A day with Autonomy (an illustration with made-up customers and no real numbers)
8:55 am. Priya bought a serum 42 days ago and has looked at moisturisers twice. Autonomy predicts she's due for a refill. She's always bought at full price and reads her email in the evening. Decision: a refill reminder by email at 7 pm, mentioning the moisturiser she viewed. No discount. Meanwhile: other customers are left alone today. Some were contacted twice this week, some are better off without a message, and one has an open complaint. Next morning the Live screen shows how many were contacted and how many bought, and Proof shows the extra profit against the untouched group so far, with its range, or "not sure yet".

Getting started is safe
- Weeks 0 to 3: a read-only audit of your data and Shadow mode on your own history. It decides but sends nothing. You see exactly what it would do and why, and what it expects to earn (a forecast, not proof).
- Then: Approve mode for one win-back goal, email first, with a small budget. You approve each batch.
- When the Proof page shows real extra profit, with the low end of the range above zero: consider Autopilot.

Honest status: it has not run with a real business yet, so start in Shadow mode, risk-free. What to expect: well-run AI marketing typically adds 3-10% in real, measured extra profit. That is general guidance, not a result from your business. We will show you your real number, with its range. Small shops cannot measure a lift that small in a few weeks: at 50,000 customers the smallest lift one 2 to 4 week window can reliably tell from zero is about 18% ([ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md](../v3/ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md) section 5c), so the Proof page says "not sure yet" until it has enough.

The ask: give us your customer data and one goal. In a few weeks of Shadow mode you'll see what it would do, why, and what it expects to earn, before a single message goes out. There is no price list yet; terms are agreed with you for the pilot.
