## 1. Vendor AI agents missing promises, agentic project cancellation, agent-washing, and GenAI pilot failure (Gartner, MIT NANDA, McKinsey, BCG)

### Takeaway
The "45% of martech leaders say vendor AI agents fall short" stat is real and traceable to a Gartner press release dated 29 Oct 2025 (n=413, fielded June-August 2025). It sits alongside strong evidence that buyers are sceptical of AI claims in general: Gartner's 40%+ agentic cancellation forecast, the MIT "95%" headline (preliminary report, contested method) and BCG's "60% see minimal value". Gartner itself says half the blame is the buyer's own data and stack readiness, not only vendor over-promising.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner survey of 413 martech leaders, June-August 2025 (press release 29 Oct 2025): 45% of martech leaders with AI agents in pilot or production say existing vendor-offered AI agent capabilities fail to meet their expectations of promised business performance. Title of the release: "Gartner Survey Finds 45% of Martech Leaders Say Existing Vendor-Offered AI Agents Fail to Meet Their Expectations of Promised Business Performance." — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-29-gartner-survey-finds-45-percent-of-martech-leaders-say-existing-vendor-offered-ai-agents-fail-to-meet-their-expectations-of-promised-business-performance)
- Same survey: 81% of martech leaders are piloting or implementing AI agents; 89% of those with agent initiatives expect significant business benefits; half say their technical and data stack is not ready for agent deployment; half cite a technical talent shortage. Gartner analyst Aparajita Mazumdar: CMOs "must also look inward" and fix gaps in technical and data infrastructure. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-29-gartner-survey-finds-45-percent-of-martech-leaders-say-existing-vendor-offered-ai-agents-fail-to-meet-their-expectations-of-promised-business-performance); readiness framing also in [CMSWire recap](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-marketing/before-you-buy-the-marketing-agent-build-the-martech-foundation/) (secondary; says only 40% report readiness across talent, technical and data foundations - different framing, treat as secondary) and [MarTech.org](https://martech.org/cmos-are-buying-ai-that-their-organization-isnt-ready-for/)
- Gartner prediction (press release June 2025, analyst Anushree Verma): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027, citing escalating costs, unclear business value and inadequate risk controls. Gartner also says only about 130 of the thousands of vendors claiming agentic AI are real; the rest are "agent washing" (rebranded assistants, RPA, chatbots). This is a forecast, not a measurement. — [Outlook Business summary](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2027-says-gartner); [W.Media, 27 Jun 2025](https://w.media/over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-could-face-the-axe-by-end-of-2027-gartner/); [MarTech.org](https://martech.org/gartner-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-fail-making-humans-indispensable/)
- The poll behind the agentic context: Gartner January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar attendees - 19% had made significant investment in agentic AI, 42% conservative investment, 8% none. Webinar-attendee sample, not a representative survey. — [W.Media](https://w.media/over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-could-face-the-axe-by-end-of-2027-gartner/)
- Gartner (survey of IT application leaders, press release 30 Sep 2025): only 15% are considering piloting or deploying fully autonomous AI agents (title only seen in search; detail not read). — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-09-30-gartner-survey-finds-just-15-percent-of-it-application-leaders-are-considering-piloting-or-deploying-fully-autonomous-ai-agents)
- MIT NANDA "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (July 2025, preliminary): headline that 95% of organisations see no measurable P&L return from genAI initiatives. Method counts are reported inconsistently: Computing.co.uk says 150 interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 public deployments; other summaries say 52 organisations interviewed, 153 senior leaders surveyed, 300+ public initiatives. Cite as a preliminary research programme, not a universal failure rate. — [Computing.co.uk](https://computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/mit-report-95pc-corporate-generative-ai-pilots-fail); [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/Articles/2025/08/19/MIT-Report-Finds-Most-AI-Business-Investments-Fail-Reveals-GenAI-Divide.aspx); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/mit-report-documents-genai-pilot-roi-gap-e0924d7d)
- BCG "The Widening AI Value Gap" (press release 30 Sep 2025): about 5% of companies are "future-built" and achieve substantial AI value; about 35% are scalers; about 60% are laggards with minimal measurable value despite investment. Agentic AI is already about 17% of total AI value and expected to reach about 29% by 2028 (per secondary summary). Sample details not retrieved. — [BCG press release](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings); [BCG PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-October-2025.pdf)
- McKinsey "The state of AI in 2025": 88% report regular AI use in at least one function (78% a year earlier); about two-thirds have not begun scaling AI enterprise-wide; 62% are at least experimenting with agents; 39% report any enterprise-level EBIT impact. - Forrester "State of AI for B2C Marketers, 2025" (11 Sep 2025, CMO Pulse Survey Q1-Q2 2025): paywalled; 90% of B2C marketing executives plan to increase AI investment in the next year. - IBM CMO Study 2025 (IBM IBV with Oxford Economics, 1,800 CMOs and CSOs, 33 geographies, March-May 2025): see India section for the Indian cut. — [IBM India newsroom](https://in.newsroom.ibm.com/2025-07-09-India-Marketing-Leaders-Under-Pressure-to-Deliver-Profitability,-See-AI-as-Key-Growth-Lever-IBM-CMO-Study-2025)
- Haus 2026 Marketing Decision Confidence Index: 78% of leaders say they are being pressured to integrate AI marketing tools, but only 51% feel confident explaining AI-driven ROI to the board (n=500, see section 2). — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/haus-survey-half-of-marketing-leaders-cant-explain-roi-measurement)

### Inferences
- The strongest sceptic-facing fact for sales conversations: nearly half of martech buyers who already run vendor agents say the promised performance did not materialise, so buyers will discount generic "agentic" claims and reward vendors who show proof (holdouts, rupee-denominated incremental results).
- Gartner's own diagnosis (half lack data/stack readiness and talent) means a product that includes a data-readiness check and handles the modelling itself addresses the buyer's stated blocker, not only the vendor-quality complaint.
- The MIT 95% and Gartner 40% figures are widely quoted but weak evidence (preliminary report; forecast). Use them as context for sentiment, not as hard market facts.

### Gaps
- Could not read the Gartner press release page directly (fetch blocked); numbers rely on search summaries plus consistent secondary coverage. Verify the 45% base: it is 45% of those with agents in pilot or production, not of all 413.
- No marketing-function-specific McKinsey figure retrieved. BCG sample size not retrieved. Forrester B2C trust and incrementality figures are paywalled.
- No independent replication of the 45% figure found.

## 2. Proving ROI, CFO scepticism, and measurement confidence

### Takeaway
Only about half of senior marketers say they can prove marketing's value and get credit (Gartner 2024); CFOs are the most-cited sceptics (40%); 2025-26 surveys show measurement confidence "stalled", with roughly two-thirds naming proving incremental ROI as the top challenge. Most data is US/Europe, not India.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner survey, 378 senior marketing leaders, April-May 2024 (press release 18 Sep 2024): only 52% say they can both prove marketing's value and receive credit for business outcomes; 40% name the CFO as the most sceptical executive of marketing's value (CEO 39%); 47% say marketing is still seen as an expense rather than a strategic investment. — [Business Wire release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240918141146/en); [Brand Innovators](https://brand-innovators.com/cmos-still-struggling-to-prove-their-value-gartner/)
- Gartner: only 40% of marketing leaders say their data and analytics met expectations for influence and demonstrable ROI; those leaders are said to be 2.4x more likely to cut or plan to cut budgets (date and sample not retrieved). — [Decision Marketing](https://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/views/how-to-use-data-and-analytics-to-prove-marketings-roi)
- Gartner 2025 CMO Spend Survey (402 CMOs/marketing leaders in North America, UK and Europe, Feb-Mar 2025, release 12 May 2025): budgets flat at 7.7% of company revenue; 59% say budget is insufficient to execute strategy; paid media averages 30.6% of budget; CMOs face in-year cuts. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-12-gartner-2025-cmo-spend-survey-reveals-marketing-budgets-have-flatlined-at-seven-percent-of-overall-company-revenue); [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/marketing-budgets-hold-77-2025-gartner-cmo-survey/1920581)
- Perion / Advertiser Perceptions "Bridging the Divide" (167 senior marketers, US and Canada, 28 Aug-10 Sep 2025): only 22% strongly feel they have data to prove marketing's value to finance; CMOs frame growth as revenue, CFOs as profitability and cost control. — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/marketing-tech/only-22-of-marketers-feel-sure-they-can-justify-spend-to-the-cfo)
- TransUnion / EMARKETER "Marketing Measurement Confidence" (196 US marketers, July 2025, released 21 Oct 2025): 60% say internal stakeholders question their metrics at least sometimes; about 29% say up to 20% of budgets were reallocated or put at risk by doubts about measurement; 67.4% name proving incremental ROI as top challenge; barriers are siloed/incomplete data (49%), cross-channel deduplication (48%), walled gardens (41%); 62% have some confidence in metrics, 54% no change YoY, 14% declined. — [TransUnion newsroom](https://newsroom.transunion.com/new-transunion-research-reveals-marketers-confidence-in-measurement-has-stalled/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/marketing-measurement-confidence-stalls-despite-data-growth/); [MarTech.org](https://martech.org/marketers-say-their-trust-in-measurement-is-stalled/)
- Haus 2026 Marketing Decision Confidence Index (500 US senior paid-marketing/finance decision-makers at firms with at least $11M paid media, online, January 2026, published March 2026): only half can explain their measurement approach to the board; 49% say their measurement programme is built to measure business-outcome impact; 74% have killed or scaled back a marketing idea for lack of measurement confidence; 78% believe at least 10% of spend is wasted due to insufficient measurement (EMARKETER) while Haus's own page says 35% believe more than 20% of budget is inefficiently allocated but cannot prove it (different questions). Haus is an incrementality-testing vendor, so the survey is vendor-sponsored. — [Haus index](https://haus.io/pages/2026-marketing-decision-confidence-index); [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/haus-survey-half-of-marketing-leaders-cant-explain-roi-measurement); [EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/confidence-marketing-measurement-falters-budgets-climb)
- LinkedIn (Australian B2B marketing leaders): 98% say improving CFO understanding of marketing ROI is key to securing future budgets. — [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/linkedin-finds-cmos-pressured-to-prove-roi-amid-uncertainty)
- Unattributed "28% of marketers consistently measure ROI" appeared in one result and could not be traced - excluded.

### Inferences
- "Proving incremental ROI to finance" is the most consistently quantified pain (about 67% top challenge, about half unable to explain measurement to the board). That maps directly onto a rupee-denominated, CI-bearing Value Proof Pack aimed at CFOs.
- All CFO-scepticism data found is marketer-reported (what marketers say CFOs think), not CFO-reported.

### Gaps
- No CFO-side survey (CFOs reporting their own trust in marketing) found for 2024-26.
- No India-specific ROI-proof or CFO data found.
- Share of campaigns run without a control group: no survey found (see section 3).

## 3. Experimentation, incrementality adoption, MMM revival, signal loss

### Takeaway
Incrementality testing is now mainstream in stated intent (about half use it, most trusted method in one vendor survey), but I found no survey on the share of campaigns run without control groups, nor on under-powered or slow tests.

### Cited Findings
- EMARKETER / TransUnion (July 2025 fieldwork, 196 US brand and agency marketers): 46.9% (press release rounds to 47%) plan to invest in MMM over next 12 months; 35% expect to increase multi-touch attribution investment; 27.6% rate MMM the most reliable methodology (MTA 19.4%, unified solutions 18.9%); 50% adopting AI/ML to automate reporting. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/marketing-measurement-confidence-stalls-despite-data-growth/); [EMARKETER MMM](https://www.emarketer.com/content/nearly-half-of-us-marketers-plan-invest-mmm-over-next-year)
- Same EMARKETER/TransUnion data as reported by search summaries: 52.0% use incrementality testing/experiments; 36.2% plan to invest in it; 27.6% say expanding incrementality testing is a top priority. The 27.6% number is attributed to two different questions across EMARKETER pages [UNVERIFIED - check original]. — [EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/incrementality-takes-center-stage--there-s-still-work-done-)
- Haus (500 US senior decision-makers, Jan 2026): 60% trust independent incrementality testing most, vs MMM 40% and in-platform reporting 37%. Vendor-sponsored. — [EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/incrementality-testing-earns-marketers--top-trust)
- Forrester blog (Brad Haag, July 2026): adoption of MMM and incrementality testing rose substantially YoY, yet 49% of B2C marketing decision-makers in 2026 still say analytics findings don't translate into action (snippet truncated; full wording unconfirmed). — [Forrester blog](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/author/brad_haag/)
- Practitioner note (not survey): control groups are commonly 5-10% of audience, which often leaves tests under-powered; Google Conversion Lift allows 1-50% holdbacks. — [PPC Land holdout study](https://ppc.land/holdout-study/)

### Inferences
- Demand for causal measurement is rising and buyers rank randomised testing as the most trusted method; this supports positioning on randomised hold-outs rather than modelled attribution.
- Most figures describe advertising (paid media) measurement in the US, not CRM/retention campaign measurement, so transfer to CVM is by analogy.

### Gaps
- Share of CRM or lifecycle campaigns run without control groups: not found in any survey.
- Under-powered or slow-test prevalence: not found as survey data.
- Signal loss survey statistics: not specifically retrieved (only walled-garden barrier 41% above).

## 4. Data readiness, data prep time, talent shortage

### Takeaway
Data and talent readiness is a recurring top blocker: roughly half of martech leaders say their stack is not agent-ready, and Salesforce's State of Marketing says only about a quarter are fully satisfied with data connectivity. Data-prep-time stats found are old.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner (Q3 2024 / July 2024 survey of data management leaders, sample reported inconsistently, one source says 248): 63% of organisations either lack or are unsure they have the right data management practices for AI. Gartner also predicts 60% of AI projects lacking AI-ready data will be abandoned through 2026 (a prediction). — [Freevacy summary](https://www.freevacy.com/news/gartner/gartner-reveals-60-of-ai-projects-with-data-issues-will-fail/6155)
- Gartner martech survey (413 leaders, June-Aug 2025): half cite stack/data unreadiness and half cite technical talent shortage (see section 1). — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-29-gartner-survey-finds-45-percent-of-martech-leaders-say-existing-vendor-offered-ai-agents-fail-to-meet-their-expectations-of-promised-business-performance)
- Salesforce State of Marketing, 10th edition (2026; vendor survey): only 26% of marketers completely satisfied with data connectivity; 98% hit barriers to personalisation, data issues most common; marketers with satisfactorily unified data are more likely to use AI agents (60% more likely globally; 1.6x in India release). Global summary: 75% adopted AI, 84% still run generic campaigns, 69% struggle to respond promptly. Global sample size not retrieved. — [Salesforce ANZ blog](https://www.salesforce.com/au/blog/marketing-trends-ai-era/); [Salesforce India release](https://www.salesforce.com/in/news/press-releases/2026/07/07/india-marketers-ai-adoption-unified-data-customer-engagement/)
- TransUnion / EMARKETER July 2025: siloed or incomplete data is the top barrier to accurate measurement (49%). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/marketing-measurement-confidence-stalls-despite-data-growth/)
- Data prep time (old): Anaconda 2020 survey - about 45% of data scientists' time on preparation; Anaconda 2022 - about 51% on preparing, cleansing or visualising data and 18% on selecting and training models; Blue Hill Research (186 analysts, date not retrieved) - 28% of analysts' time. No 2024-26 figure found. — [O'Reilly/Datanami via oreil.ly](https://oreil.ly/jDVWF); [Solutions Review](https://solutionsreview.com/business-intelligence/report-data-analysts-use-28-of-time-on-data-preparation)
- Mid-market survey cited by Analytics8: only 14% report full data readiness (source and date not verified). [UNVERIFIED] — [Analytics8](https://www.analytics8.com/?p=2010)

### Inferences
- A readiness diagnostic and a data-light entry point (works from transactions and campaign logs) addresses a documented blocker, but no data shows how many Indian B2C firms are blocked.

### Gaps
- No 2024-26 quantified data-prep-time survey; no data-scientist shortage figure specific to India or marketing; no India data-readiness share.

## 5. Over-messaging, contact fatigue, opt-outs, discount waste

### Takeaway
Consumer surveys consistently show message overload driving unsubscribes, but they are mostly US, small-sample and vendor-commissioned. The discount-waste stat ("52% of promotions go to people who would pay full price") is from 2018, so out of the requested window.

### Cited Findings
- Optimove 2025 Consumer Marketing Fatigue Report: 70% unsubscribed from at least three brands in the last three months due to message overload; 36% from six or more. Sample size reported inconsistently (329 US consumers per Optimove summary vs n=2,000 per a clean.email compilation) [sample UNVERIFIED]. — [Optimove report](https://www.optimove.com/resources/downloads/optimove-insights-2025-consumer-marketing-fatigue-report); [clean.email](https://clean.email/blog/insights/email-subscription-fatigue-statistics)
- Optimove 2025 holiday survey: 82% of consumers unsubscribe from firms that send texts and emails while they shop online. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/407917/holiday-extremes-shoppers-like-email-but-are-sho.html)
- Attentive "2026 State of Loyalty and Retention": 43% of shoppers unsubscribe due to message fatigue; 43% of those liked the brand but received too many messages. — via [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/412584/keeping-customers-close-ways-to-keep-them-ways-t.html) [sample and fieldwork date not retrieved]
- Adobe (undated in results): 80% name "too many messages" as top reason to unsubscribe or turn off notifications [year UNVERIFIED]. — [Adobe](https://business.adobe.com/th_th/resources/sdk/reimagining-customer-journey-management.html)
- EMARKETER: US consumers overwhelmed by brand emails and texts (figures not retrieved). — [EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-consumers-overwhelmed-by-brand-emails-texts)
- Discount waste (OUTSIDE WINDOW, 2018): Forrester Consulting for Revionics, consumers in US, UK, France, Germany and Brazil - 52% of weekly or monthly promotions go to customers who would have paid full price; 37% of those receiving unneeded offers said the offer had neutral or negative impact; vendor-commissioned. — [Refrigerated & Frozen Foods](https://refrigeratedfrozenfood.com/articles/94355-study-finds-retailers-offer-wrong-promotions-to-wrong-customers-at-the-wrong-time)
- India D2C unit economics (vendor-style, secondary write-up): Growww Tech 2026, 200+ Indian D2C brands - first order unprofitable for 78%; Meta CAC averaged Rs 502 in 2026 vs Rs 380 in 2025; brands with repeat rates above 25% had 3.4x higher margins than those below 15%. — [Startupwired, 10 Sep 2026](https://startupwired.com/2026/09/10/indian-d2c-startups-making-customer-acquisition-profitable/) [UNVERIFIED - primary report not seen]

### Inferences
- Over-contact and subsidy waste are well described qualitatively and by consumer surveys, but no marketer-side survey quantifying discount waste in 2024-26 was found. The report writer should avoid presenting the 2018 figure as current.

### Gaps
- No India-specific opt-out or contact-fatigue survey found (CleverTap and MoEngage channel benchmarks were gated); no 2024-26 discount-waste or uplift-vs-propensity survey.

## 8. What buyers say they'd pay for; outcome-based pricing appetite

### Takeaway
Buyer surveys show moderate, not dominant, appetite for outcome-based pricing: roughly 27-31% of enterprise buyers prefer it (Futurum), with consumption pricing preferred more. No marketing-specific or India-specific pricing survey found.

### Cited Findings
- Futurum 1H 2025 (enterprise decision-makers; sample not retrieved): for genAI functionality 38% prefer consumption-based pricing, 31% outcome-based, 20% per-user-per-month, 11% bundled in core licence. — [Futurum press release](https://futurumgroup.com/press-release/enterprise-software-buyers-prefer-consumption-and-outcome-based-pricing/)
- Futurum 1H 2026: 43% prefer consumption-based, 27% outcome-based; fewer than one in five prefer classic per-user pricing. — [Futurum press release](https://futurumgroup.com/press-release/are-outcome-based-and-hybrid-ai-pricing-models-rewriting-the-vendor-playbook/)
- BCG 2025 (B2B software pricing in the era of AI): 40% of IT buyers cite seat reduction as primary lever to cut software spend; buyers perceive more value in expertise and complete task execution than in isolated workflow steps. — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/rethinking-b2b-software-pricing-in-the-era-of-ai)
- Nevermined (vendor blog, source unnamed): 39% expect outcome-based pricing to become standard for AI agent services. [UNVERIFIED] — [Nevermined](https://nevermined.ai/blog/pricing-model-experimentation-ai-statistics)
- Haus index: 74% have killed or scaled back marketing ideas for lack of measurement confidence, a proxy for willingness to pay for proof (section 2).

### Inferences
- A pay-on-measured-incremental-rupees model would appeal to a minority (about a quarter to a third) as a stated preference; many buyers prefer predictable consumption pricing. A hybrid (platform fee plus performance share) is a reasonable hypothesis, not tested by these surveys.

### Gaps
- No marketing-buyer or India outcome-pricing survey; no data on what CRM/CVM buyers would pay for specific capabilities (uplift, proof packs).
