## 1. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: timelines, notice, consent, consent managers, fiduciary duties, SDFs, retention, children, breach, penalties, and implications for profiling, model training and hold-outs

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 Nov 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)) with a three-tranche rollout: Board and definitions immediately, consent-manager registration (Rule 4) at 12 months (about 13 Nov 2026, not yet in force as of 10 Oct 2026), and nearly all substantive duties (notice, security, breach, erasure, children, SDF, rights) at 18 months (about 13 May 2027). A January 2026 MeitY proposal to compress the 18-month window to 12 months was floated but, as of late Sept 2026, no amending notification was found. DPDP has no "legitimate interest" basis, so marketing, profiling and outreach effectively run on specific, withdrawable consent, with the fiduciary carrying the burden of proving valid consent.

Consent and notice
- Section 6(1): consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action"; no pre-ticked boxes or bundling. Section 6(4): withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. Withdrawal does not make earlier processing unlawful; the fiduciary must stop processing within a reasonable time — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/lawful-processing-and-consent-under-dpdp-act); [DPDP wiki consent FAQ](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/faq/consent-and-notice/)
- Section 6(10): burden is on the fiduciary to prove notice was given and valid consent obtained — [SCC Online on WhatsApp chatbot/DPDP](https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=391696)
- DPDP has no GDPR-style "legitimate interest"; direct marketing is not a Section 7 legitimate use and must rest on consent — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-the-inconspicuous-absence-of-legitimate-interest)
- Rule 3 notice: stand-alone, plain language, itemised categories of personal data and specific purposes, plus means to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board — [DPDP Rules explainer](https://dpdpactindia.in/dpdp-rules/); [Scrut](https://www.scrut.io/post/dpdp-rules); [Bitraser](https://www.bitraser.com/article/dpdp-rules-2025.php)
- Marketing consent should be separate from transactional (order-update) consent; prior consent for one channel does not authorise WhatsApp marketing — [DPDPA.com WhatsApp guide](https://www.dpdpa.com/blogs/whatsapp_business_dpdpa_compliance_messaging_apps.html); [MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-compliance-india-dpdp-meta-policy-2026)
- Legacy consent (Section 5(2)): for consent given before commencement, the fiduciary must give a notice "as soon as reasonably practicable"; processing may continue until the principal withdraws. No fixed statutory deadline (secondary-source quote of the text) — [Watchdog Security legacy-data page](https://watchdogsecurity.io/dpdp/legacy-data-notice); [Ecorpit legacy-data remediation](https://ecorpit.com/dpdp-legacy-data-remediation-consent-backfill-india-2026/)

Consent managers (Rule 4, Schedule 1 per sources)
- Must be a company incorporated in India, net worth at least INR 2 crore, sound finances/management, adequate technical/operational/financial capacity; independent certification that the interoperable platform meets Board-published standards — [dpdpa.com Schedule 1](https://dpdpa.com/schedule/schedule1.html); [Maheshwari & Co](https://www.maheshwariandco.com/blog/consent-manager-under-dpdp-rules/); [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/consent-manager-registration-opens-soon-technical-and-legal-readiness-checklist-for-aspiring-consent-managers)
- Role: Board-registered single point of contact for giving, managing, reviewing and withdrawing consent via an accessible, transparent, interoperable platform; consent records kept 7 years and "data-blind" operation (readiness-guide claim, secondary) — [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-consent-manager-framework-india/); [Ecorpit readiness checklist](https://ecorpit.com/dpdp-consent-manager-framework-readiness-india-2026/)
- Using a consent manager is not mandatory for a data fiduciary; a CM is not the same as a cookie-consent platform — [Vinsys](https://www.vinsys.com/blog/dpdp-act-compliance-deadline-nov-2026-for-consent-manager) (secondary)
- Claim that foreign vendors such as OneTrust cannot register without an Indian subsidiary: single trade-news claim, unverified — same Vinsys / search set

Security, breach, retention, children, SDF
- Rule 6 security baseline: encryption/masking/tokenisation, access control, logging and monitoring, backups; log retention of at least one year (sources disagree whether this sits in Rule 6/Seventh Schedule or Rule 8(3)) — [Scrut](https://www.scrut.io/post/dpdp-rules); [Sansa Legal on retention](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-build-a-data-retention-and-erasure-schedule-under-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- Rule 7 breach: intimate affected principals without delay (via user account/registered channel); intimate the Board without delay and give a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware (or longer if Board allows on written request), covering facts, causes, mitigation, responsible person findings and the principal-intimation report. One source (TCS advisory page) wrongly implies a 72h clock for principals — [DPDP Rule 7 text page](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule7.html); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-data-breach-notification-timeline/); [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-breach-notification)
- Rule 8 erasure: for specified classes (e-commerce, online gaming, social media above user thresholds) data is presumed to be erased after 3 years of principal inactivity, with at least 48 hours' prior notice to the principal — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-build-a-data-retention-and-erasure-schedule-under-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [Veritect](https://veritect.ai/digital-data-ai-law/dpdp-rules-2025-phased-rollout) (search summary; thresholds not captured)
- Children (Section 9, Rule 10-12): verifiable parental consent; no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children (s.9(3)); limited exemptions in Fourth Schedule — [Veritect children's playbook](https://veritect.ai/digital-data-ai-law/dpdp-childrens-data-compliance-playbook); [Assurtiv](https://assurtiv.com/child-data-protection-rules-2025-under-dpdp-act/)
- SDF (Section 10, Rule 13): designation is by separate central notification (none made yet); duties are annual DPIA and audit with report to the Board, resident DPO, independent data auditor, algorithmic due diligence, cross-border restrictions — [dpdpa.com Rule 13](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule13.html); [PrivacyGlobal](https://www.privacyglobal.org/blog/dpdp-rule-13-significant-data-fiduciaries-duties); [Matters.ai](https://www.matters.ai/compliance/dpdp/dpdp-rules-2025)
- Research/statistical exemption (s.17(2)(b) plus Rules standards): exempt only if no individual decision-making and standards are met. Whether it covers AI model training is argued, not settled; commentators reject blanket permission — [Consent.in](https://www.consent.in/blog/dpdp-research-exception); [Law School Policy Review, Jan 2026](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2026/01/13/publicly-available-data-under-the-dpdp-act-the-limits-of-exemptions-in-ai-driven-processing/)

Penalties (Schedule to the Act; maxima, set by Board per case)
- INR 250 crore: failure to take reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)); INR 200 crore: failure to notify breach (s.8(6)) and children's obligations (s.9); INR 150 crore: SDF obligations (s.10); INR 50 crore: residual contraventions; Centre can amend the Schedule, capped at 2x — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/data-privacy-breach-enforcement-penalties-under-the-dpdp-act); [Perfios](https://perfios.ai/?p=67875); [dpdpa.com schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html). Note: some compliance blogs say "up to INR 250 crore" generally; the INR 250 crore ceiling attaches specifically to the security-safeguard duty. Penalties apply from 18-month tranche per Grant Thornton flyer.

### Inferences
- A marketing decisioning product is a data fiduciary or (more likely, for brand clients) a processor to the brand; the brand bears consent/notice duties but the product's "consent ledger + purpose tags + audit evidence" is exactly what the brand needs to discharge the s.6(10) burden of proof. This is the product's core compliance advantage.
- Since there is no legitimate-interest basis, win-back / retention outreach to lapsed customers needs a recorded, purpose-specific, channel-specific consent; "inferred" or legacy consent is risky after May 2027. A "legacy-consent remediation / re-permissioning campaign" feature is a clear opportunity (Section 5(2) notice).
- Hold-out groups: no source addresses them directly. Not contacting people is not a processing harm, but if hold-out membership is derived from profiling and stored, it is still personal data subject to purpose limitation, retention and erasure; hold-outs must also honour erasure/withdrawal. (Inference — no cited source.)
- Training models on historic customer data: unless fiduciary got consent for that purpose or relies on the contested research exemption (which fails if outputs drive individual decisions, which marketing decisioning does), a purpose tag "model training / personalisation" needs to be in the notice. Safest design: purpose-tagged consent for personalisation and model training, per-customer lineage so withdrawn customers can be removed from training sets.
- Profiling is not banned for adults but the SDF "algorithmic due diligence" and DPIA duties may bite if a brand is designated an SDF (large data volumes); the product should generate DPIA/audit artifacts.
- 12-month compression risk: product roadmap should be able to meet 13 Nov 2026 as a stress case.

### Gaps
- Primary Gazette text of Rules not read (WebFetch blocked); rule-by-rule numbering conflicts exist between secondary sources (e.g. which rule holds the one-year log retention; Rule 12 vs 13 for SDF DPIA). Verify against G.S.R. 846(E).
- Exact user thresholds and class list for Rule 8 / Third Schedule not captured; whether quick-commerce/D2C apps meet "e-commerce entity" thresholds is not established.
- Technical interoperability standards for consent managers: not found.
- No authoritative source found on how DPDP treats hold-out/control groups, A/B experimentation, or internal analytics/profiling for retention marketing.
- Whether the Jan 2026 timeline compression was ever finalised: no amending notification found as of late Sept 2026; recheck Gazette/MeitY.
- Whether any entity has been notified as SDF: none found.

## 2. TRAI (TCCCPR 2018 and 2024-26 amendments), WhatsApp, RBI/IRDAI/SEBI marketing rules, dark patterns

### Takeaway
SMS/voice marketing is already under a hard, enforced telecom regime: DLT-registered headers/templates, a Digital Consent Acquisition (DCA) registry, whitelisted URLs/APKs/callback numbers, 140 (promotional) and 1600 (service/transactional) number series, and escalating disconnection penalties, now tightened again by the TCCCPR Third Amendment Regulations 2026 (notified 18 Sept 2026). WhatsApp sits outside DLT but under Meta policy and DPDP consent. Financial-sector marketing adds RBI/IRDAI/SEBI layers, and CCPA's dark-pattern rules apply to consent UX.

### Cited Findings

TCCCPR 2018 amendments (Feb 2025)
- TRAI amended TCCCPR 2018 in Feb 2025: consent/verification tightening; inferred consent valid only for duration of contractual relationship; explicit consent for a commercial transaction valid 7 days from acquisition (per JSA summary as reported by search); call-management apps may not block/tag designated commercial series (Reg 34A); revised Schedule I on sender/telemarketer verification — [JSA Prism Feb 2025](https://www.jsalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/JSA-Prism-Telecommunications_February-2025_TCCCPR-amendments.Final_.pdf); [TRAI PR 11 of 2025](https://cms.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-02/PR_No.11of2025.pdf); [Fonearena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/446114/trai-tcccpr-2025-tougher-rules-spam-calls-sms.html); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/trais-crackdown-on-spam-calls-and-ai-driven-telemarketing). Note the "7 days" figure applies to the validity of explicit consent in the specific sense reported; confirm against regulation text.
- Penalty ladder (Feb 2025): first violation, bar on sender's outgoing services for 15 days; repeat violations, full disconnection for one year and blacklisting across access providers — [Fonearena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/446114/trai-tcccpr-2025-tougher-rules-spam-calls-sms.html)
- Series: promotional calls via 140 series; service/transactional via 1600 series (secondary sources conflict on naming: one says DoT introduced '160' in Aug 2024) — [Fonearena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/446114/trai-tcccpr-2025-tougher-rules-spam-calls-sms.html); [HDFC Fund SEBI/TRAI page](https://www.hdfcfund.com/information/sebi-trai-directions-and-guidelines-commercial-communications)

Digital Consent Acquisition (DCA)
- June 2023 TRAI direction ordered access providers to build a unified DCA platform for seeking, maintaining and revoking consent on a DLT ledger, scrubbed by all access providers; consent-seeking messages go through common short code '127xxx', must state purpose, scope and the brand (PE) name, and only whitelisted URLs/APKs/OTT links/callback numbers allowed; earlier-collected consent treated as invalid; BFSI principal entities onboarded first — [Deloitte alert](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/in/Documents/tax/Global%20Business%20Tax%20Alert/in-tax-gbt-TRAI-Direction-to-Access-Providers-on-Digital-Consent-Acquisition-noexp.pdf); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/quantum-brief/trai-to-implement-digital-consent-acquisition-system-to-curb-spam-in-commercial-communications-17079.htm); [IndiaCorpLaw](https://indiacorplaw.in/?p=12827); [FoxMandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/trai-mandates-fresh-consent-for-sending-promotional-messages/)
- Dec 2025: TRAI-RBI DCA pilot announced (10 Dec 2025) to fix the "legacy consent gap" for consents never migrated to the Digital Consent Registry; 9 telecom providers and 11 banks participating — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/data-privacy/trai-rbi-digital-consent-pilot/) (search summary only)
- TRAI consent covers calls/SMS only and is separate from DPDP consent — [IndiaLaw anti-spam rules](https://indialaw.in/blog/food/trai-anti-spam-rules)

Headers/templates/URL whitelisting
- From 1 Oct 2024, messages containing URLs/APKs/OTT links/callbacks not whitelisted by the sender must not be transmitted; templates with more than three variable parts need justification — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/trai-extends-deadline-for-move-aimed-at-curbing-misuse-of-messaging-services); [TRAI PR 80 of 2024](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/PR_No.80of2024.pdf); [NSDL copy of policy guidelines](https://nsdl.co.in/downloadables/pdf/2024-0123-Policy-Guidelines_to_curb_spam_SMSes_and_misuse_of_Headers_and_Content_Templates.pdf)

TCCCPR Third Amendment Regulations 2026
- Draft consultation issued 13 Mar 2026 (comments to 12 Apr, counter-comments 27 Apr); final Third Amendment Regulations notified 18 Sept 2026 per PIB/TRAI PR 119 of 2026 — [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2311989&lang=1&reg=3); [TRAI PR 119 of 2026](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/PR_No119of2026.pdf); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/trai-tightens-anti-spam-rules-brings-ai-based-detection-under-framework-126091800660_1.html)

1600 series for regulated entities

WhatsApp
- Meta Business Messaging Policy requires explicit opt-in; marketing templates need Meta approval; sources disagree whether WhatsApp traffic falls under TRAI/DLT (most say no, it does not) — [MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-compliance-india-dpdp-meta-policy-2026); [DPDPA.com](https://www.dpdpa.com/blogs/whatsapp_business_dpdpa_compliance_messaging_apps.html); [MessageCentral](https://www.messagecentral.com/en-in/blog/whatsapp-business-api-india-guide). Dark patterns / Consumer Protection
- CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 list 13 patterns (e.g. basket sneaking, subscription trap, drip pricing, confirm shaming); 5 Jun 2025 advisory told e-commerce platforms to self-audit within three months; binding force of the advisory debated; DCA notices issued to 11 e-commerce platforms — [Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/ccpa-mandates-e-commerce-self-audits-dark-patterns.html); [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/central-consumer-protection-authority-issues-advisory-to-e-commerce-platforms-for-self-audit-to-detect-dark-patterns-on-their-platforms/); [Law School Policy Review](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2025/07/11/decoding-the-ccpas-dark-patterns-advisory-binding-guidelines-or-just-soft-law/); [IFF](https://www.internetfreedom.in/dark-patterns-and-weak-remedies-why-the-ccpa-advisory-falls-short/)

### Inferences
- Any promotional send must clear a stack: DPDP consent (purpose + channel) AND TCCCPR consent/DCA registry AND DLT header/template/variable-tag validity AND whitelisted URL/callback AND, for WhatsApp, Meta opt-in + template approval. A pre-send "compliance gate" is therefore a multi-system join, ideal for an agent/rules engine with an immutable decision log.
- Template matching: because variable pre-tags and whitelist scrubbing are now enforced (messages rejected after the 60-day logger window per the Nov 2025 direction), an AI that maps a generated offer message to a registered template ID and validates variables/URLs pre-send avoids silent delivery failures and sender suspension (15-day outgoing bar).
- Retention/win-back offers aimed at lapsed customers are the highest-risk TCCCPR category: inferred consent expires with the contractual relationship; explicit-consent windows are short (reported 7 days for a transaction-linked consent). Reconsent journeys via DCA are needed.
- For NBFC/insurance clients, the product should route service vs promotional voice to 1600 vs 140 series.
- CCPA dark-patterns rules mean the product's own consent UX templates (opt-in/opt-out flows) should be dark-pattern-checked (confirm-shaming copy, nagging re-prompts).

## 3. Account Aggregator / DEPA as a model for consent managers

### Takeaway
The RBI-regulated Account Aggregator (AA) ecosystem is the operating proof that India-scale, purpose-bound, revocable, machine-readable consent artefacts work; it is widely cited as the template for DPDP consent managers. As of Sept 2026 it has passed 500 million fulfilled consents.

### Cited Findings
- Sahamati (Sept 2026, 5th AA Foundation Day): 500M+ fulfilled consents, 1,100+ regulated entities live, 2.8B+ financial accounts enabled for sharing, 310M+ accounts linked, INR 3.82 lakh crore lending in FY26; RBI recognised Sahamati as the AA ecosystem's SRO in June 2026 — [NewKerala (Sahamati release coverage)](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/indias-account-aggregator-ecosystem-facilitates-nearly-38-crore-263.htm); [Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/?p=1274421)
- FY25: NBFCs accounted for 60.08% of consents, capital markets ~35%; ecosystem of 179 FIPs, 748 FIUs, 16 AAs — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/nbfcs-lead-account-aggregator-consents-in-fy25-with-60-share-125100600872_1.html). Counts differ across Sahamati newsletters (e.g. 913 FIUs in Jan 2026; 34 crore consents in Dec 2025 vs 45 crore cumulative in June 2026 report) due to differing dates — [Sahamati newsletter Dec 2025](https://sahamati.org.in/varta-the-sahamati-newsletter-december-2025/); [Jan 2026](https://sahamati.org.in/varta-the-sahamati-newsletter-january-2026/)
- DSCI consent management paper (2025) discusses DPDP consent architecture — [DSCI](https://www.dsci.in/files/content/documents/2025/consent-management.pdf) (title only seen, content not read)

### Inferences
- AA offers a design pattern for the product's consent ledger: structured consent artefacts (purpose, data scope, duration, revocation), data-blind intermediary, signed logs. A product aligned to AA-style artefacts can integrate later with DPDP-registered consent managers (interoperability) instead of building a closed ledger.
- For BFSI clients, AA consent (data pull for lending) is distinct from marketing consent; do not conflate purposes.

### Gaps
- DEPA's formal design principles and NBFC-AA RBI master direction specifics not retrieved; no source mapping AA artefact schema to DPDP consent-manager interoperability standard (which is not yet published).

## 5. Where AI agents can turn compliance into a product advantage; existing vendors

### Takeaway
Compliance work in this domain is high-volume, rules-dense, evidence-hungry and cross-system (DPDP ledger, DCA registry, DLT templates, WhatsApp templates): exactly where agents can add value as pre-send gatekeepers, template matchers, evidence-pack assemblers, rights-request handlers and purpose taggers. Vendor landscape for DPDP consent management is forming; no consent-manager registrations exist yet (registration opens after Rule 4 starts about 13 Nov 2026).

### Inferences (agent opportunities, product features)
1. Pre-send consent gate agent: for every (customer, channel, purpose, template) decision, check DPDP ledger (purpose-specific, unexpired, not withdrawn), TCCCPR/DCA consent status and validity window, DND/preference, quiet hours, frequency caps, children flag; block and log reason. Output: signed decision record per send (the "evidence").
2. DLT template-matching agent: map LLM-generated copy to a registered template ID/header, validate variable pre-tags (post-Nov 2025 rules) and whitelisted URL/callback; propose registration of new templates; auto-select 140 vs 1600 series for voice.
3. Purpose-tagging agent: classify every data field, model feature and campaign by DPDP purpose; refuse training/scoring on records lacking "personalisation/model-training" purpose consent; maintain lineage to enable deletion from training sets.
4. DPDP evidence-pack agent: assemble notice versions shown, consent timestamps/channels, withdrawal history, suppression proofs, breach-readiness logs (72h report template), retention/erasure runs, DPIA drafts for SDF-like clients, hold-out design docs. Make the evidence pack continuous, not point-in-time.
5. Data principal rights agent: intake, identity-verify, route access/correction/erasure/nomination/grievance requests across channels; propagate erasure to suppression lists while retaining minimal suppression keys (hashed) so opted-out customers are never re-contacted (design question for counsel).
6. Legacy-consent remediation agent: segment historic customers by consent provenance and run compliant re-permissioning journeys (DCA 127xxx flow for SMS; WhatsApp opt-in; email) with Section 5(2) notices.
7. Regulatory-change watcher agent: monitor TRAI directions, Gazette (e.g., timeline compression), and update rules versioning, with before/after impact on active campaigns.
8. Dark-pattern linter for consent and offer UX against CCPA's 13 patterns.
9. Hold-out governance: treat hold-out as a documented, purpose-limited experiment: record assignment, rationale, retention, and show that hold-out customers are not harmed (e.g., no withdrawal of statutory service); not found in any source, inference only.
10. Interop path: expose consent as AA-style artefacts and plan an adapter to registered consent managers once they exist; do not pursue own CM registration unless strategic (INR 2 crore net worth, data-blind constraint conflicts with analytics use).
