## 1. Tabular foundation models (TabPFN family, TabICL, Mitra, TabDPT, LimiX, TabuLa-8B, CARTE) and causal PFNs

### Takeaway
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) are real: on TabArena they now rank at or near the top on small/medium data, and AutoGluon 1.4+ ships several of them in an `extreme` preset. But the best TFM weights (TabPFN-2.5/2.6/3) are non-commercial unless you pay Prior Labs, and the evidence for beating tuned GBDT/AutoGluon on 20k-500k-row churn data is vendor-led and weakest at the large end. Open-licence options (Mitra Apache-2.0, TabICL/TabICLv2, TabPFN-2 weights under the Prior Labs attribution licence) are the safest to productise. Causal PFNs (CausalPFN, Do-PFN) are promising research but independent evidence shows shrinkage bias and they assume ignorability; no marketing-uplift benchmark of them was found.

### Cited Findings
**TabPFN lineage and benchmarks**
- TabPFN v2 paper: Hollmann, Müller, Purucker, Krishnakumar, Körfer, Hoo, Schirrmeister, Hutter, Nature vol. 637, pp. 319-326, 9 Jan 2025. Claims to beat prior methods on datasets up to 10,000 samples, and to outperform a 4-hour-tuned ensemble of the strongest baselines in 2.8 s on classification. Trained on synthetic data only. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39780007/); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711098/); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v637y2025i8045d10.1038_s41586-024-08328-6.html)
- Independent evaluation of TabPFN v2 on 261 datasets: it beat tree-based and deep tabular models with statistically significant differences overall, but on 18 large-scale datasets CatBoost had average rank 1.89 vs 3.97 for TabPFN v2. — [A Closer Look at TabPFN v2 (arXiv 2502.17361)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17361) (as summarised by search; I did not read the paper)
- TabPFN-2.5 released 6 Nov 2025 (Prior Labs). Targets up to 50,000 rows x 2,000 features (about 20x more data cells than v2); claims it leads TabArena, beats tuned tree models, and matches AutoGluon 1.4 (4-hour tuned ensemble that includes TabPFNv2). Default TabPFN-2.5 reportedly beats default XGBoost on 100% of small/medium classification datasets (<=10k rows, 500 features) and 87% on larger ones up to 100k rows (85% for regression). Includes a distillation engine (to MLP / tree ensemble) for low-latency serving. These are Prior Labs' own numbers. — [TabPFN-2.5 report on alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.08667.md); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)
- Prior Labs "Scaling Mode" claims strong performance up to about 10 million rows, but the same vendor post says there is no evidence the gap to gradient boosting shrinks as data scales. — [Prior Labs technical report](https://priorlabs.ai/technical-reports/large-data-model) (vendor)
- TabPFN-3 (Prior Labs): Wikipedia lists a stable release dated 12 May 2026. Prior Labs changelog says up to 1M rows x 200 features natively (about 10x previous), or 100k rows x 2,000 features; Thinking mode; native text features; up to 160 classes. A Hugging Face card says up to 1M samples and <=2000 features and that it is trained purely on synthetic tabular tasks. — [Wikipedia TabPFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TabPFN); [Prior Labs changelog](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/changelog/tabpfn-3); [HF card mirror](https://huggingface.co/lsw2333/tabpfn_3)
- A Sept 2026 blog says a TabPFN-3.5 technical report was published 14 Sept 2026 (up to 1M rows; 6,000 columns recommended). Secondary source only; primary report not found. — [digitalapplied.com](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/tabpfn-3-5-tabular-foundation-model-when-to-use)
- A consulting blog reports TabPFN-3 at 1673 Elo vs 1375 for tuned XGBoost on TabArena. Unverified against the official leaderboard. — [ecorpit.com](https://ecorpit.com/tabular-foundation-models-tabpfn-vs-xgboost-enterprise-2026/)
- Inference latency trade-off: one summary notes TabPFN needed about 0.2 s vs CatBoost 0.0002 s on a 10,000-row dataset. — [search summary citing the TabPFN benchmark; see ecorpit/Prior Labs links above](https://priorlabs.ai/technical-reports/large-data-model)

**Independent benchmarks**
- TabArena (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks): living benchmark, v0.1 with 16 models incl. 3 TFMs. Findings: GBDTs remain competitive on practical datasets; deep learning catches up with ensembling under larger time budgets; foundation models excel on smaller datasets; cross-model ensembles push state of the art; TabICL and TabPFNv2 were only run on subsets because they do not apply to all datasets; validation method and HPO ensembling strongly affect rankings. — [NeurIPS 2025 abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1697e3fb412da11dc9488249f9e7bbc9-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16791v4)
- TabICLv2 (arXiv 2602.11139, Feb 2026): claims that, untuned, it surpasses RealTabPFN-2.5 (tuned, ensembled, fine-tuned on real data) on TabArena; scales to million-row datasets under 50 GB GPU memory; faster than TabPFN-2.5 (one summary: 10.6x speedup at 50k samples on H100). Appendix: binary rank 4.43 (2nd behind AutoGluon 1.5 extreme 4h at 3.83); multiclass rank 6.75, behind AutoGluon 1.5 extreme (4.00) and RealTabPFN-2.5 T+E (4.25). The 4.82-vs-5.88 overall rank figure is from an aggregator. Weights released; training code to follow. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11139); [alphaxiv summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.11139.md)
- TabDPT (Layer 6): a later version reports the highest default regression performance of any model and is close to RealTabPFN-2.5 after tuning/ensembling. A third-party Elo table (Xiaomi-TabLDM report) lists TabDPT (T+E) 1722, TabPFN-2.6 (D) 1741, RealTabPFN-2.5 (T+E) 1736, with a model "TABFM (D)" at 2019; columns (T/D/T+E) not explained in the snippet. — [TabDPT arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18164v3); [Xiaomi-TabLDM report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03880)
- LimiX: only an aggregator source found; claims evaluation on 10 large structured-data benchmarks and Apache-2.0 release. Not verified. — [aggregator listing from search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03880)
- TabuLa-8B (Llama-3-8B fine-tuned on tables): across 329 datasets zero-shot accuracy on unseen tables is more than 15 percentage points above random guessing; it is an LLM-based approach, not a rival to GBDT on pure accuracy. — [Large Scale Transfer Learning for Tabular Data](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12031v2)
- Mitra (Amazon, mixed synthetic priors; NeurIPS 2025): pretrained on a curated mixture of synthetic priors; Mitra-v2 is a 12-layer 2D transformer (75.7M params, attention across rows and columns) with 10x longer context, 3x more features; Hugging Face model card lists Apache-2.0. — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/mitra-mixed-synthetic-priors-for-enhancing-tabular-foundation-models); [HF regressor-2 card](https://huggingface.co/autogluon/mitra-regressor-2); [arXiv 2510.21204](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21204)
- CARTE: no results retrieved. TALENT benchmark: no results retrieved. xLSTM-tabular: not searched. (see Gaps)

**AutoGluon integration (directly relevant to the current stack)**
- AutoGluon 1.4.0 added RealMLP, TabM, TabPFNv2, TabICL, Mitra, and MLZero 1.0 (AutoGluon-Assistant agent). The `extreme` preset uses a portfolio of TabPFNv2, TabICL, Mitra, TabM plus CatBoost/LightGBM/XGBoost; only for datasets with <=30,000 samples (larger ones fall back to best_quality). Best with a CUDA GPU (ideally 32+ GB VRAM); downloads weights at fit time (needs internet). — [AutoGluon 1.4.0 what's new](https://auto.gluon.ai/1.4.0/whats_new/index.html); [AutoGluon 1.4 foundational-models tutorial](https://auto.gluon.ai/1.4/tutorials/tabular/tabular-foundational-models.html)
- AutoGluon 1.5 added RealTabPFN-2.5, which carries a non-commercial licence plus a HuggingFace auth step and is not in any preset. AutoGluon 1.6.0 adds more TFMs: TabPFN-3, TabDPT-Turbo, TabPFN-2.6, TabICLv2 and Nori. — [AutoGluon 1.6.0 what's new](https://auto.gluon.ai/dev/whats_new/v1.6.0.html) (via search summary; page itself not fetched)

**Licensing / cost**
- TabPFN-3 weights: tabpfn-3-license-v1.0 prohibits using the model, derivatives and outputs for commercial or production purposes (internal evaluation allowed). TabPFN-2.5, 2.6 and 3 weights are non-commercial; TabPFNv2 weights and the code are under the "Prior Labs License" (Apache 2.0 plus attribution) which allows commercial use. The hosted API is permitted for commercial use under its ToS; on-prem or private-cloud commercial deployment needs a licence. A Commercial Enterprise License includes a fast inference engine, distillation, support; no public price (contact sales). — [Prior Labs pricing](https://priorlabs.ai/pricing); [Prior Labs GitHub README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/priorlabs/tabpfn@main/README.md); [HF tabpfn_3 card](https://huggingface.co/Prior-Labs/tabpfn_3)
- Wikipedia describes TabPFN-3 source code as "modified Apache"; relationship to the weights licence unconfirmed. — [Wikipedia TabPFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TabPFN)
- Mitra weights Apache-2.0 per AutoGluon tutorial and HF card. — [AutoGluon tutorial](https://auto.gluon.ai/1.4/tutorials/tabular/tabular-foundational-models.html)
- Pre-trained on synthetic data only for TabPFN and Mitra, so no customer-data privacy concern in pretraining; inference on client data would still need a data-processing decision if the hosted API is used (Indian client data leaving the country). This last point is my inference, not a cited finding.

**Causal PFNs / CATE**
- CausalPFN (Balazadeh et al.; NeurIPS 2025 slides): transformer trained once on simulated data-generating processes satisfying ignorability; estimates causal effects in a single forward pass; reports best average performance on IHDP, Lalonde, ACIC heterogeneous/average effect benchmarks and calibrated uncertainty. — [arXiv HTML 2506.07918v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07918v2); [NeurIPS slides](https://neurips.cc/media/neurips-2025/Slides/118013.pdf)
- Do-PFN (Robertson, Reuter, Guo, Hollmann, Hutter, Schölkopf; NeurIPS 2025): pretrained on synthetic data from many causal structures incl. interventions, predicts p(y|do(t),x) from observational context, evaluated on synthetic and semi-synthetic data; roughly 7.3M-parameter transformer per an aggregator. — [NeurIPS 2025 abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ff997469ac66cf893c4183efeb22212a-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2506.06039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06039)
- A later comparison paper (via hyper.ai summary, a single secondary source) says Do-PFN and CausalFM systematically shrink effect estimates toward zero; CausalPFN had best average rank among causal foundation models. A March 2026 paper (Melnychuk et al., arXiv 2603.12037) reports prior-induced confounding bias in PFNs used as Bayesian ATE estimators and proposes a one-step correction. — [hyper.ai summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2609.03003); [arXiv 2603.12037](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12037)
- TabPFN-2.5 as a T-learner on the RealCause semi-synthetic benchmark (RCT-derived data, PEHE metric): PFN-based CATE methods occupy the top seven places on the leaderboard (vendor report). — [TabPFN-2.5 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)
- Marketing-uplift reference point without TFMs: UpliftBench (Apr 2026 single-author preprint, unverified) on Criteo Uplift v2.1 found an S-learner with LightGBM had the best Qini (0.376) vs T-/X-learners and EconML Causal Forest. — [arXiv 2604.06123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06123)

### Inferences
- For the product's current AutoGluon stack, the lowest-friction path is upgrading AutoGluon and enabling its TFM portfolio (TabPFNv2, TabICL/TabICLv2, Mitra are commercially usable; TabPFN-2.5/2.6/3 and RealTabPFN-2.5 are not without a Prior Labs licence). The `extreme` preset's 30k-sample cap and GPU requirement fit small clients but not the 200k-500k end, where GBDT or large-data TFMs (TabICLv2, TabPFN-3 under licence) would be needed.
- The TFM benefit is probably largest for small clients (20k-50k customers), small-subgroup models, and per-arm outcome models for uplift (T-learner), where data per arm is thin; it is least proven for large, wide, highly imbalanced churn tables. Gains are likely a few rank positions, not a step change, since GBDT ensembles remain competitive.
- Causal PFNs should be treated as a research watch item, not a replacement for randomised holdouts: they assume ignorability and show shrinkage/prior bias. Randomised treatment (which the product can create via holdouts) makes simple TFM T-/X-learners a more defensible use than amortised causal inference.
- Inference latency and GPU needs matter for nightly scoring of 500k customers; Prior Labs' distillation engine is enterprise-only.

## 2. LLM agents for data preparation (schema matching, feature engineering, cleaning, leakage, text-to-SQL, AI data scientists)

### Takeaway
LLM schema matching and feature-proposal methods work well enough to be useful as suggest-and-confirm assistants, but published evidence is on public benchmarks, not messy Indian retail/fintech CRM exports. Autonomous "AI data scientist" agents have improved sharply (MLE-bench medal rates went from about 17% to well above 30% on subsets) yet still fail often on hard tasks, and text-to-SQL benchmarks are partly broken by annotation errors. The robust pattern is human-in-the-loop approval of mappings and labels, with deterministic checks (existing Data Doctor, leakage checks) as guardrails.

### Cited Findings
**Schema matching / mapping**
- Magneto (NYU VIDA, PVLDB 2025; arXiv 2412.08194): two-phase design, small-language-model candidate retrieval then LLM reranking; introduces a new benchmark incl. real biomedical datasets; the paper's Figure 1 says the best traditional technique reaches at most 0.45 MRR. Magneto's own MRR was not retrieved. Code: github.com/VIDA-NYU/magneto-matcher. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08194v1); [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.08194.md)
- SemStruct (2026, arXiv 2605.30729) uses Magneto as a baseline on the Valentine benchmark with MRR and Recall@GT; numbers not retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30729)

**Automated feature engineering**
- CAAFE (NeurIPS 2023): LLM proposes features as Python code with explanations from a dataset description; improved 11 of 14 datasets, tied 2, lost 1; mean ROC AUC 0.798 to 0.822 (authors compare to random forest 0.782 vs logistic regression 0.754). Framed as a complement to AutoML. — [NeurIPS 2023 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72592); [arXiv 2305.03403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03403v1)
- LLM-FE (arXiv 2503.14434): evolutionary program search with LLM proposals and data-driven feedback; authors claim consistent gains over CAAFE and OCTree and that about 45% of discovered features are "complex" (vs negligible for baselines). Self-reported; no head-to-head against AutoGluon found. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14434)

**Data cleaning**
- AutoDCWorkflow (EMNLP 2025 Findings): LLM generates OpenRefine cleaning workflows from a table plus a stated analysis purpose; benchmark of 142 purposes over 96 tables, six topics; Llama-3.1-8B answer-generation F1 rose from 0.2201 to 0.5181. Cocoon is another LLM cleaning system reported to beat existing tools; LLMs struggle with ambiguous data-quality issues. — [alphaxiv overview of 2412.06724](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2412.06724v3); [Moonlight review](https://themoonlight.io/review/autodcworkflow-llm-based-data-cleaning-workflow-auto-generation-and-benchmark); [emergentmind LLMClean](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/llmclean)

**Leakage detection**
- Draoui et al. (ACM, DOI 10.1145/3808199): static slicing plus structured LLM prompts to detect preprocessing and overlap leakage in Kaggle/GitHub notebooks; F1 up 22% (preprocessing leakage) and 15% (overlap leakage) over prior state of the art; slicing beat end-to-end prompting. — [CiteDrive listing](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/improving-data-leakage-detection-in-machine-learning-notebooks-through-static-slicing-and-structured-llm-prompts/)
- "Talking Trees" (arXiv 2509.21465) appendix: leakage stress test of an LLM-agent tabular model; notes that memorised preprocessing pipelines and validation-split overfitting were leakage routes it did not isolate. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21465)
- LLM contamination on tabular benchmarks: larger, closed-source models are more likely to have seen benchmark datasets (Ronval, Dupont, Nijssen, IDA 2025). — [IDA 2025 PDF](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~pdupont/pdupont/pdf/ida_25.pdf)

**Text-to-SQL**
- Spider 2.0 (original paper, 2024): o1-preview-based code agent solved 21.3% of tasks vs 91.2% on Spider 1.0 and 73.0% on BIRD. — [alphaxiv Spider 2.0](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2411.07763v2) (via search summary)
- Annotation audit (VLDB 2026): error rates of 52.8% (BIRD Mini-Dev) and 62.8% (Spider 2.0-Snow); correcting a BIRD Dev subset shifted scores by -7% to +31% relative and moved ranks by up to 9. Spider 2.0 repo notes an evaluation-suite fix on 2025-10-29 that re-scored methods. — [Pervasive Annotation Errors (rmarcus dbscholar)](https://rmarcus.info/dbscholar/papers/14621); [Spider2 GitHub](https://github.com/xlang-ai/Spider2)
- Current leaderboard numbers for Spider2-Snow/Lite or BIRD could not be verified (an aggregator showed 96.7% for an unspecified subset; do not rely on it). — [benchmarklist.com](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/spider_2_0/)

**AI data scientist agents**
- MLE-bench (OpenAI, Oct 2024): best setup o1-preview + AIDE reached at least bronze medal level in 16.9% of 75 Kaggle competitions; AIDE/GPT-4o 8.7%, AIDE/Claude-3.5-Sonnet 7.6%. — [arXiv 2410.07095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.07095); [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/openais-mle-bench-tests-ai-coding-agents)
- Later systems: Famou-Agent 2.0 with Gemini-3-Pro 80.3% medal rate at 24h on MLE-bench Lite; official-leaderboard entries of 33.3% (Famou-Agent, Gemini-2.5-Pro) and 40.0% (Leeroo, Gemini-3-Pro-Preview) any-medal on a larger set. Subsets, budgets and models differ, so not comparable. — [ScienceFlow 2608.14354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14354); [Hierarchical skill accumulation 2606.30911](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30911)
- MLZero / AutoGluon-Assistant (Amazon, NeurIPS 2025): 92% success on its own 25-task Multimodal AutoML Agent Benchmark, 86% on MLE-bench Lite, six gold medals there; 10th at 2024 Kaggle AutoML Grand Prix and the only automated agent to score points. Company-reported. Shipped as part of AutoGluon 1.4. — [Amazon Science blog](https://www.amazon.science/blog/autogluon-assistant-zero-code-automl-through-multiagent-collaboration); [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119504); [arXiv 2505.13941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13941)
- DS-STAR (Google Research): planner + verifier + router agent; integrated with Gemini-2.5-Pro raises DABStep hard-level accuracy from 12.70% to 45.24%; also evaluated on KramaBench and DA-Code. Even the improved hard-task accuracy is under 50%. — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/ds-star-a-state-of-the-art-versatile-data-science-agent/); [arXiv 2509.21825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21825)
- DA-Code (agentic data-science code-generation benchmark) and DAComp (full data-intelligence lifecycle benchmark) exist but numbers were not retrieved. — [DA-Code](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.07331); [DAComp](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.04324)

### Inferences
- Guided setup (mapping client tables to the schema) is the best-matched use: Magneto-style retrieval plus LLM reranking would present top-k column candidates with confidence, and a human confirms. Public benchmarks do not cover Indian CRM/transaction exports with Hinglish/abbreviated column names, so a pilot on the product's past onboardings is required before trusting it.
- Label construction (churn/value) is semantic and high-stakes; agents should draft the definition and show example customers, while the product's leakage checks remain deterministic. LLM leakage detection in the literature is code/notebook-oriented and moderately accurate (F1 gains of 15-22% over prior tools, not near-perfect), so LLMs should supplement, not replace, rule-based leakage checks.
- Feature-engineering agents (CAAFE/LLM-FE) show about +0.02 AUC on public sets; with TFMs and GBDTs already strong, the practical value for the product is more likely in proposing business features (recency/frequency/discount-sensitivity) with explanations than in raw lift.
- Benchmarks for text-to-SQL are unreliable (high annotation error), and enterprise accuracy is far lower than Spider 1.0 suggests; for a product that controls its schema, constrained query templates or a semantic layer is safer than free-form SQL.
- Failure-mode and human-in-the-loop: published agent success rates on hard tasks are below 50%, so autonomous end-to-end pipelines on client data are not yet dependable; propose-then-approve and sandboxed execution with automatic checks (the existing Data Doctor) is the evidence-aligned design.

### Gaps
- Magneto's headline numbers, current Spider 2.0 / BIRD leaderboard scores, and any enterprise-CRM-specific schema-matching result were not retrieved.
- No documented failure-mode taxonomy or independent reliability study for Google's Colab Data Science Agent was found.
- No study of human-in-the-loop effect sizes (time saved, error rates) for LLM-assisted schema mapping or label definition.
- No head-to-head of LLM feature engineering vs AutoGluon with TFMs.
- No research found on LLM handling of Indian-language/transliterated column names or product catalogues.

## 3. Generative marketing content measured causally (variants as arms, vendors, synthetic personas, compliance)

### Takeaway
Rigorous public evidence that LLM-written copy lifts outcomes is thin and modest: the clearest randomised result (a large consumer platform) shows about +1% engagement only when outputs are reranked by a reward model, and unfiltered LLM subject lines did worse than the rule-based baseline. Vendor claims (Persado, Aampe) are large but self-reported. Synthetic-persona pre-testing matches averages in some marketing studies but under-represents variance and subgroups, so it is a screening aid at best. Note that MoEngage acquired Aampe (June 2026), shrinking the independent-vendor landscape that the product integrates with.

### Cited Findings
**Experimental evidence on LLM-generated messages**
- "Let AI Entertain You" (arXiv 2312.12457): A/B tests of generated notification/subject text vs rule-based. Best system (generator + evaluator with reward model) gave +1% sessions, +0.4% weekly active users, +1% ads revenue; plain ChatGPT generation gave -6.5% sessions and extraction-style prompting -2.8%; 10-word cap performed best and prompting alone did not reliably enforce length. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.12457)
- Mercari (arXiv 2508.20024): about 1 million lapsed users split equally; control got a fixed template title from the first recommended item; treatment got LLM-generated thematic titles; one-week test, one email per user; tracked send, open, item click-through and buyer conversion. Effect sizes not retrieved. Also registered as a trial (socialscienceregistry 12739). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20024); [AEA registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/12739)
- AlchemyWorx: observational claim that more subject-line variants correlates with higher opens/clicks; not randomised. — [alchemyworx.com](https://alchemyworx.com/blog/can-using-ai-generated-subject-lines-increase-lift/)

**Vendors**
- Persado: Chase press claims up to 450% CTR lift (other placements 50-200%); Persado claims its Motivation AI beats human or other-AI copy 96% of the time; site/earlier listings cite average conversion lifts of 30% and 41%; mortgage lender case ~140% click lift and >25% more loan applications. No design, sample size or randomisation details found; all vendor/press. — [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/persado-gets-five-year-deal-with-jp-morgan-chase); [Persado](https://www.persado.com/?p=13938)
- Phrasee/Jacquard and Movable Ink: no independent controlled evidence retrieved.
- Aampe: one agent per user, each keeps a persistent model of the person. MoEngage announced acquisition of Aampe on 24 June 2026; customers named include ZenBusiness, Taxfix, Grab, Swiggy. Taxfix (vendor-side account): beat a four-year-refined rule-based CRM by 50%, +40% revenue vs global holdout, breakeven in 30 days. Aampe states 14-43% relative GMV lift and 21% fewer unsubscribes, "results vary by use case". — [MoEngage announcement](https://www.moengage.com/blog/moengage-acquires-aampe/); [Aampe site](https://aampe.com/ai-marketing-personalization)
- Aampe research: an arXiv preprint "Behavioural Effects of Agentic Messaging" (Jeunen and Wheeler, Aampe) reports a two-month randomised trial vs a rule-based campaign system focused on unsubscribe behaviour and conversion timing; venue (RecSys 2025 / ECIR 2026 / UMAP 2026 claims) not verified. Aampe's holdout guidance recommends three groups: no-message, business-as-usual, and Aampe-only, and notes a small Aampe group underperforms because agents learn from each other. — [arXiv 2512.17462](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17462v1); [Aampe KB holdout](https://kb.aampe.com/user-guide/how-do-i-know/experiments/holdout-part1)
- CleverTap has a Template Builder Agent that generates WhatsApp template copy from a prompt (Pro and Ultimate plans, WhatsApp Direct only, not Authentication templates). Its SMS product has AI-generated content, character counting, encoding detection, URL shortening. No source found on generative content features from MoEngage or Netcore. — [CleverTap docs](https://docs.clevertap.com/docs/whatsapp-template-creator); [CleverTap SMS](https://clevertap.com/messaging-channels/sms/)

**Synthetic personas / silicon samples**
- Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People (Park et al., Nov 2024): agents built from interviews with 1,052 people matched General Social Survey answers at about 85% of the participants' own two-week test-retest consistency; comparable on personality and experiment replications, smaller accuracy gaps across racial and ideological groups than demographic-only agents. Built from long interviews, which a brand typically lacks. — [arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/simulating-human-behavior-with-ai-agents)
- NIM study of LLM "digital twins" vs 461 real soft-drink buyers: matched real choices about 79% of the time but overstated brand choice likelihood, gave systematically more positive brand attitudes, and was significantly less varied. — [NIM](https://www.nim.org/en/publications/detail/leaving-insight-to-digital-twins)
- Sarstedt, Adler, Rau, Schmitt (Psychology & Marketing): results vary considerably by domain; silicon samples most promising for upstream qualitative pretesting and pilots, more cautious for main studies. NIM overview notes most validations come from non-marketing domains. — [Columbia Business School listing](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/using-large-language-models-generate-silicon-samples-consumer-and-marketing); [NIM guidelines](https://www.nim.org/en/publications/detail/using-silicon-samples-in-marketing-research)
- Padua thesis: Gemini, Claude and GPT-4o as conjoint respondents on real FMCG data showed a trade-off between distributional fidelity and directional fidelity (thesis, low weight). — [thesis.unipd.it](https://thesis.unipd.it/handle/20.500.12608/110145)
- Ball et al. (arXiv 2502.16280): across 14 models, LLM-generated data fails to reproduce human response variance, especially across demographic subgroups. Li, Li and Qiu (arXiv 2507.02919): structural inconsistency and homogenisation of minority opinions in silicon samples. Both are on US/political survey items, not Indian consumers. Training-data bias toward Western English-speaking populations noted in a synthetic-social-data review. — [Ball et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16280v1); [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02919); [review (arXiv 2510.19952, via Moonlight summary)](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/synthetic-social-data-trials-and-tribulations)

**Compliance / India DLT**
- TRAI framework requires entity, header and template registration on DLT platforms by registered Indian entities; carriers scrub messages against approved templates in real time and block non-matching text; registering on one operator's portal syncs across networks; header suffixes -T/-S/-P/-G applied from May 2025 (Telerivet's description); template approval typically 3-7 business days (one provider). Mostly vendor documentation, not TRAI orders. — [Plivo](https://www.plivo.com/docs/sms/concepts/dlt-registration-process); [Telerivet](https://www.telerivet.com/blog/india-sms-compliance-trai-dlt-registration-and-tcccpr-guide); [Message Central](https://www.messagecentral.com/en-in/blog/dlt-content-templates-explained-an-insight-into-various-types-of-texting)
- MSG91 offers a "DLT AI" tool that pulls approved templates from the DLT portal and maps variables ({#var1#}-style) so API messages pass scrubbing. — [MSG91](https://msg91.com/help/how-to-use-dlt-ai-and-what-is-the-use-of-dlt-ai)
- No source found on whether AI-generated content needs separate approval, or on automated brand/claim-check tools for Indian regulators.

### Inferences
- Because SMS content must match a pre-registered DLT template, generative variation for SMS is constrained to selecting among approved templates or filling variables; genuinely free-form generation fits WhatsApp (also template-approved by Meta), email, push and in-app better. Generative "arms" for SMS therefore mean pre-registering a small set of LLM-drafted variants and randomising among them.
- The strongest design supported by the evidence is: LLM generates N candidate variants, a reward/quality model and a compliance filter pre-screen them, and the survivors are randomised against a control template with a no-message holdout. Raw LLM output without filtering has shown negative lift in at least one large test.
- Aampe/MoEngage convergence means integration partners are adding 1:1 agentic decisioning natively. The product's differentiation is the causal layer (holdouts, uplift, incrementality proof); the Aampe holdout recommendation (no-message, BAU, agent-only) mirrors what the product could offer as measurement-as-a-service across vendors.
- Synthetic personas are best used for cheap pre-screening of message concepts (rank-ordering, brand-safety, tone) with Indian-language calibration against past campaign results, not for predicting absolute lift; evidence shows low variance and positive bias, and no validation on Indian consumers was found.

### Gaps
- Mercari effect sizes and the Aampe preprint's numbers were not retrieved; no independent third-party RCT of Persado, Phrasee/Jacquard or Movable Ink found.
- No evidence on LLM-generated copy in Hindi/regional languages or Hinglish, nor on WhatsApp template approval pass rates.
- No official TRAI position on AI-generated content; DLT sources are vendor docs.
- No study validating synthetic personas against Indian consumers or against actual campaign A/B results.
- Nothing retrieved on brand/claim-check automation accuracy (e.g., for financial or health claims).

## 4. LLMs explaining model outputs and LLM save-desk / agent-assist

### Takeaway
LLM-written narratives of model outputs are rated convincing and improve comprehension, but automated checks show they can contradict the underlying SHAP numbers, so narratives need to be generated from structured facts with automated claim verification (or templates). For agent assist, the best causal evidence is Brynjolfsson et al.'s field study (+14-15% productivity, larger for novices), while Amazon Connect/Q in Connect evidence is vendor case-study level only.

### Cited Findings
**Grounded explanation**
- SHAPstories (Martens et al., arXiv 2309.17057): over 90% of a general-audience survey found LLM-generated SHAP narratives convincing, and the narratives improved accuracy on comprehension questions in a credit-scoring setting. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17057)
- "How good is my story?" (arXiv 2412.10220): two-LLM pipeline where one model writes the narrative from a SHAP table and another extracts claims that are checked against the true values (rank/sign accuracy); reports hallucination problems, and a secondary summary says models sometimes "self-correct" contradictory inputs by favouring internal knowledge over the SHAP values. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10220v1); [alphaxiv summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2412.10220)
- Parcalabescu and Frank: many faithfulness tests for natural-language explanations measure output self-consistency, not faithfulness to the model's internals; CC-SHAP proposed. — [arXiv 2311.07466v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07466v4)
- TalkToModel and ConvXAI use template-based generation to keep explanations faithful; conversational interfaces risked increasing user over-reliance vs dashboards. — [CEUR-WS Vol-4061 paper 1](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4061/paper1.pdf)
- Vectara hallucination leaderboard (HHEM-2.3, 7,700+ articles, updated 11 May 2026): best models at about 1.8% (antgroup finix_s1_32b), 3.1% (gpt-5.4-nano), 3.3% (gemini-2.5-flash-lite) hallucination rate in document summarisation. This is a grounded-summary figure; open-recall tests are far worse per secondary sites. Secondary blog rankings that disagree with the leaderboard should be ignored. — [Vectara GitHub](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard)
- Citation-grounding techniques for RAG faithfulness were not searched in depth (see Gaps).

**Agent assist / save-desk**
- Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, "Generative AI at Work": 5,179 customer-support agents (NBER 2023), AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average and 34% for novice/low-skilled workers with minimal effect on experienced workers; also improved customer sentiment and employee retention. QJE version (vol. 140, pp. 889-942, 2025) reports 15% on 5,172 agents. — [NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161); [arXiv 2304.11771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v1); [CIRJE PDF](https://www.cirje.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/research/workshops/emf/paper2024/emf1209.pdf)
- Amazon Q in Connect: Anthropic-hosted case study reports 10-15% time savings per contact for Orbit, 61 languages (metric unspecified). D3Clarity cites 15% AHT reduction and up to 2x contacts/hour from unnamed cases; Intuit's 20% AHT reduction is attributed to Amazon Connect broadly (unverified). No primary customer study isolating Q in Connect's effect found. — [Anthropic customer story](https://www.anthropic.com/customers/amazon-q-in-connect); [Dunamis blog](https://getdunamis.com/dunamis-blog/amazon-connect-part-6-transforming-customer-service-operations)

### Inferences
- "Reasons per customer in business language" is the closest current feature. The evidence supports generating narratives from structured reason codes (templates or constrained LLM output) and running an automated claim-check (sign/rank of each cited factor vs SHAP) before display; free-form LLM paraphrase invites silent contradiction of the model.
- For a save-desk, the strongest transferable result is the novice-uplift pattern: a call-centre assist that surfaces the model's churn reason, the recommended offer (from uplift scores) and a script should help less-experienced agents most. But Brynjolfsson's setting was chat support, not retention calls, and no study measured retention-offer save rates with LLM assist.
- Over-reliance risk: conversational explanation interfaces increased over-reliance in one comparison, so the UI should show confidence and uncertainty, not only fluent text.

### Gaps
- No retention/save-desk-specific randomised evidence for LLM assist (save rate, offer cost); no Indian call-centre study (multilingual voice).
- Hallucination rates for narratives over tabular model outputs specifically (beyond the SHAP-narrative papers) and citation-grounding techniques (attribution to source rows, constrained decoding, claim-verification) were not researched in depth.
- Amazon Connect numbers are vendor case-study level; no primary AWS customer page was readable.
