# 02 - The Customer: Buyers, Workflows, Trust and Market

Research date: 2026-10-19. Most figures come from vendor reports and trade press. Treat vendor-published numbers as directional. Anything marked "(unverified)" came from one secondary source only.

---

## 1. How CRM / lifecycle / growth teams actually work

**The calendar is the operating system.** Lifecycle job postings (Braze, Klaviyo and SFMC shops, 2025-2026) centre on "own the campaign calendar": turn briefs into sends and journeys, set cadence and audiences, QA and schedule, and **chase approvals** from creative, merchandising, product, analytics and agencies. A typical week:

- **Calendar blasts.** Segment campaigns like "Diwali sale to buyers from the last 180 days". Most of the volume and most of the stress.
- **Always-on journeys.** Welcome, abandon, post-purchase and win-back flows, built once in a journey builder and rarely tuned.
- **Production and approvals.** Litmus 2023: collecting feedback (35%) and buy-in (32%) were top blockers. Firms with 500+ staff spend 99% more time on reviews (older Litmus data). AI cut production time: by Litmus 2025, only 6% of teams need more than 2 weeks per email, against 62% in 2024. Approval is still the bottleneck.
- **Reporting.** Opens, clicks and attributed revenue in a weekly deck. Incremental lift only in mature teams.

**Agencies.** Enterprises and many Indian D2C brands outsource creative and sometimes CRM execution. In 2025, 39% of CMOs planned agency cuts (Gartner). AI is absorbing agency work like variant writing and calendar execution, which gives us a wedge and agencies a reason to partner.

**Small businesses.** The owner does it all (Constant Contact 2026: 47% run their own social media). In India that means WhatsApp broadcasts from a BSP tool (AiSensy, Interakt, WATI). No calendar, segments or measurement.

## 2. Biggest pains, 2025-2026

| Pain | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Flat budgets, pressure to prove ROI | Gartner CMO Spend: budgets 7.7% of revenue (2025) and 7.8% (2026). 56% say budget is insufficient for their 2026 strategy. AI already takes 15.3% of the marketing budget, but only 30% of CMOs say their AI readiness is mature. |
| Personalisation they can't execute | MoEngage 2025 (847 B2C marketers): hyper-personalisation is the #1 challenge (52.6%), ahead of budget. Salesforce State of Marketing (10th ed., secondary summaries): 84% still run generic campaigns. Braze 2026: 60% use AI for cross-channel personalisation, but nearly half lack tools to coordinate channels. |
| Data access | Salesforce: only 51-58% have full access to commerce, sales or service data. Teams with unified data are reportedly 60% more likely to deploy AI agents. |
| Measurement distrust | Forrester 2026: B2C leaders' confidence in measuring business impact falls about 7 points to 72%. IAB State of Data 2026: 60-75% of US buyers say their measurement falls short. EMARKETER/TransUnion 2025: 52% use incrementality tests, but at a basic level. |
| Shelfware | Gartner 2025: marketers use only 49% of their martech capability. |
| Volatility | Forrester: 64% of B2C execs expect a more volatile 2026, 52% tighter budgets, 51% fewer staff. |
| Customer trust gap | Braze 2026: 93% of marketers say AI helps them understand customers. Only 53% of consumers agree. |

**In short:** more personalisation with fewer people and flat budgets, on a half-used stack, defended to finance with attribution numbers nobody trusts.

## 3. How they are measured (and judged)

- **CRM managers:** attributed revenue or "% of revenue from owned channels", repeat rate, churn, list growth, opt-outs, deliverability. Day to day: opens, clicks, conversions.
- **CRM heads and CMOs:** incremental revenue, CLV, CAC payback, margin. The 2025-2026 trend is from last-click attribution to **lift vs a holdout** (Optimove and Pedowitz push "incremental revenue vs control" as the headline KPI).
- **The politics:** attributed numbers look big. Finance disputes them because email takes credit for purchases that would have happened anyway. Honest incrementality can make the operator's dashboard *smaller*. It helps us with the CFO and CMO but threatens the operator. Position it as "proof you can defend to finance".

## 4. Attitude to AI autonomy: what they will and won't delegate

The clearest data is **StackAdapt "The AI Delegation Gap"** (Aug 19, 2026; NewtonX; 500 marketers across NA, EMEA and APAC):

| Level of autonomy | % comfortable |
|---|---|
| AI recommends actions | 90% |
| AI prepares actions for human approval | 89% |
| AI acts within human-defined rules | 78% |
| AI acts fully autonomously, *even with proven performance* | 50% |

Behaviour lags stated comfort. Only 6% almost always act on in-platform AI recommendations (unverified). The top reasons for ignoring them are generic output (42%) and misfit with strategy (22%). Only 22% of EMEA advertisers feel very confident judging the AI's calls. Ahrefs (Sept 2026): 66% trust AI content only after human review. Forrester predicts agentic adoption will *slow* in 2026 and tells marketers to "clarify desired levels of autonomy".

**What vendors shipped (a demand signal):**

- **Klaviyo K:AI Marketing Agent** (Sept 2025). Drafts campaigns and flows from a URL. *You review and approve.*
- **MoEngage Merlin Custom Agents** (Jun 2026). Marketers set audiences, channels, content rules and **budget limits** first, then watch every step. Autonomy is a dial.
- **Braze Forge 2026.** Decisioning Studio Go (self-serve RL for creative, timing and frequency), "Agentic Standards" pre-send QA (pass/warn/fail), and MCP access. Coverage headline: "speed and autonomy require governance".

**What builds trust (synthesis, most important first):**

1. **A visible holdout with lift in money.** Haus 2026: 60% of senior leaders trust incrementality tests most (MMM: 40%). An agency founder: "you convince them to trust the outcomes, then build backward."
2. **Marketer-set guardrails.** Budget, frequency caps, quiet hours, exclusions, discount limits, brand voice.
3. **An autonomy ladder.** Recommend, then approve, then auto inside rules, then full auto, per segment. Start on about 10% of the base.
4. **A plain-English reason for each decision.** "WhatsApp at 7pm: she opens in evenings, email is dormant."
5. **A pre-send QA gate** with instant pause or undo.

**They keep:** brand voice, new creative, discount budgets, tentpole campaigns, VIPs. **They hand over:** who, when, channel, frequency, which variant, follow-up timing, suppression.

## 5. Market segments for a general autonomous agent

| | Small business / owner-led | Mid-market (approx. $10M-$500M revenue; 20K-5M customers) | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who buys | Owner | Head of CRM / Growth, with sign-off from CMO, founder or CFO | CMO + CIO + procurement + security + legal |
| Team | 0-1 person | 2-10 person CRM team, often an agency too | 20-200, plus agencies |
| Data | Shopify / POS / a spreadsheet | Shopify or app events + CRM + a warehouse, often messy | CDP or warehouse, mature |
| Current tools | Klaviyo, Mailchimp, AiSensy/WATI | Klaviyo, MoEngage, CleverTap, WebEngage, Braze Core | SFMC, Adobe, Braze, Hightouch |
| Autonomy appetite | High ("just do it for me") | Medium-high, if they can see the holdout and guardrails | Low to medium; governance-heavy |
| Willingness to pay | $20-$500/mo | $1.5K-$15K/mo | $100K-$1M+/yr |
| Sales cycle | Self-serve, same day | 1-3 months | 6-12+ months, with 6-14 people on the buying committee |

**Best fit: mid-market B2C with 50K-5M customers** (D2C, consumer apps, retail chains, fintech, edtech, subscriptions):

- Enough volume for bandits and uplift models to learn.
- A real CRM owner who feels the personalisation gap but lacks staff.
- ROI pressure, so a holdout report sells.
- Decisions in weeks, not quarters.
- Priced out of enterprise decisioning (OfferFit, Braze Decisioning Studio Pro, Hightouch).

Small businesses want autonomy most but have thin data and low budgets. Serve them later with a self-serve WhatsApp "lite" tier in India. Enterprise has money but blocks on governance and integration. Enter later via the warehouse or as a decisioning layer over the incumbent sender.

## 6. Buying, budgets and pricing

**Budget lines.** The spend comes from the martech/CRM tool budget (owned by the CRM head, approved by the CMO), from agency budget that is being reallocated, and sometimes from the "AI transformation" pot (Gartner: 15.3% of marketing spend). In India, the founder or CBO often signs off directly on mid-market deals.

**Prices in the market today:**

| Model | Examples |
|---|---|
| Per contact / profile | Klaviyo: free up to 250, about $20/mo for 500, about $400/mo for 25K, about $720/mo for 50K email profiles. Email + SMS at 25K is about $945/mo. |
| Per MAU, annual contract | Braze: Core about $30K-$80K/yr (50K-250K MAU), Pro $80K-$250K, enterprise $250K-$1M+, plus implementation of $20K-$300K. Vendr average is about $93.5K (third-party estimates). CleverTap: about $500/mo entry, $1.25 per 100 MAU (Essentials). MoEngage: from about $999/mo; India mid-market INR 7-15 lakh/yr. WebEngage: INR 4-6 lakh/yr at 30K MAU. |
| Per message (pass-through) | WhatsApp marketing in India costs ₹0.8631 per message (from Jan 2026) + 18% GST, about ₹1.02. BSP markup adds ₹0.10-0.30. There is no volume discount on marketing messages. |
| Platform fee + seats (India SMB) | AiSensy from ₹999/mo; WATI from ₹2,499/mo (real bills run 30-50% higher); Interakt from about ₹999-2,499/mo, with AI add-ons around ₹10K/mo. |
| Outcome-based | Common in support AI: Intercom Fin $0.99 per resolution, Zendesk about $1.50-2.00 per verified resolution (May 2026), Agentforce $2 per conversation. **I found no established public outcome or revenue-share pricing for marketing decisioning.** OfferFit-style deals are enterprise contracts. |

**Pricing implication.** Buyers know three units: contacts or MAU, messages, platform fee. Outcome pricing is normal in support AI, but in marketing it hits the attribution fight. The only defensible outcome unit is **incremental revenue vs a randomised holdout agreed up front**, which v1 already measures. A pragmatic hybrid: platform fee by customer count, plus pass-through message cost, plus an optional capped success fee on verified incremental profit.

## 7. Adoption killers

1. **Integration effort.** Integration and APIs rank #2 among martech replacement factors (MarTech 2024). Enterprise implementation costs $20K-$300K. Mid-market deals stall if first value takes more than about 2 weeks.
2. **Fear of spamming.** WhatsApp numbers get quality-rated and blocked, and opt-outs are permanent. Visible frequency caps and fatigue suppression are needed. Aampe advertises 21% fewer unsubscribes, so "less spam" sells.
3. **Brand risk.** One bad message to a VIP ends the pilot. Forrester predicts a third of brands will damage trust with premature genAI.
4. **Loss of control and job threat.** Pitch "decision-maker leverage" (Braze's framing), not replacement.
5. **Attribution disputes.** Without an agreed holdout, ROI claims are dismissed.
6. **Incumbents are adding agents.** Braze bought OfferFit for $325M (Mar 2025). MoEngage bought Aampe (Jun 2026; claims 200B decisions a week; customers include Swiggy and Grab). Klaviyo, Iterable (Nova) and Salesforce all ship agents. 1:1 RL decisioning is becoming a feature of platforms buyers already pay for.

**India angle.** WhatsApp-first: GoKwik 2026 covers 26B messages from 1,800+ D2C brands, and 83% of festive-quarter WhatsApp orders came from first-time buyers. Buyers are price-sensitive (lakhs, not $100Ks), founders decide fast, and they compare us with MoEngage, CleverTap, WebEngage, Netcore and BSPs. MoEngage says growth comes from enterprises leaving SFMC and Adobe, so Indian vendors win globally on cost and speed. DPDP compliance is coming but rarely blocks deals today.

## What this means for us

### Do
- Target **mid-market B2C first** (50K-5M customers), with a WhatsApp-first India path.
- Make the **holdout-verified incremental revenue/profit report** the hero of the product and the pitch.
- Ship an **autonomy ladder** per goal or segment, with one-click pause or undo.
- Put **marketer-set guardrails** up front: budget, fatigue caps, quiet hours, VIP exclusions, discount ceilings, brand voice.
- Give a **plain-English reason per decision** plus a weekly digest: what I did, why, what it earned.
- Sit **on top of existing tools** (Klaviyo, MoEngage, Braze, BSPs, the warehouse) with under 2 weeks to first value.
- Price as platform fee + pass-through messages + optional capped success fee on verified incremental profit.
- Sell "fewer, better messages" alongside revenue.

### Don't (or not yet)
- Don't lead with "fully autonomous". Only about 50% accept it even with proof. Let autonomy be earned.
- Don't go enterprise first: 6-12+ month cycles, governance, entrenched RL incumbents.
- Don't own brand voice, new creative or tentpole campaigns at launch. Draft them for approval.
- Don't price on revenue share alone. Attribution fights kill renewals.
- Don't build our own ESP or BSP.
- Don't make attributed revenue the headline number.

### Open questions
- Is the buyer the CRM operator (who loses attributed credit) or the CMO, founder or CFO?
- What customer-base size lets bandits and uplift beat a good rule-based calendar within about 30 days?
- Should we be a standalone dispatcher or a decisioning layer over incumbents?
- How do we differentiate against MoEngage+Aampe and Braze+OfferFit: tool neutrality, profit optimisation, sleeping dogs, India pricing, goal-to-ROI loop?
- Will buyers accept the short-term revenue cost of a 5-10% holdout?
- Is a ₹2-10K/mo SMB tier viable on thin data?
- Who are the first 10 design partners? This desk research has few first-hand operator voices, so interviews are needed.
