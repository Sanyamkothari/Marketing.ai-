# Enterprise engagement and decisioning stack: what an outside vendor can read, what randomised data already exists, who already decides

Research date: 21 Oct 2026. Target client: a big company (food delivery and consumer apps such as Zomato, Swiggy, Uber Eats; SaaS; banks; telecoms; insurers; large retailers). Earlier scope was small Klaviyo shops. The short small-shop findings are kept in the last section. Write-back mechanics (how to trigger a send) are in [connectors/02_apis_and_write_back.md](../connectors/02_apis_and_write_back.md) and are not repeated. The real-data findings that frame this note are in [REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md](../../../v2/REAL_DATA_RESULTS.md): about one purchase per customer so per-person learning starves, and no causal lift from history without control groups.

## How reliable this note is

The page fetch tool could not reach any vendor site in this session (DNS failures for braze.com, mailchimp.com, help.klaviyo.com and others). Everything below comes from web search extracts of vendor docs, a few third-party posts (marked) and one SEC filing listing. Nothing was tested against a live account. I found nothing public about the actual martech stacks at Zomato, Swiggy or Uber Eats, so no claim is made about them.

## The three questions

### 1. Do enterprises already have randomised data we can learn causal lift from?

**Yes, often, and this is the biggest difference from small shops.** Almost every enterprise engagement tool has a standing random control group, and the mature decisioning tools keep randomised exploration on purpose.

| Source of randomisation | Who has it (exact feature) | What it gives us | Main catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standing global control (send versus no send)** | Braze Global Control Group (1% to 15% of users). MoEngage Global Control Group (admin picks a percentage or uploads a list, one per workspace, 2% in the docs example). CleverTap System Control Group (one per account, covers the whole base, applied to every campaign by default per older docs). WebEngage Universal Control Group (request via the customer success manager). Adobe Journey Optimizer global control groups and a journey-level holdout (limited availability, Sept 2026 release notes). SAS 360 Direct automatic holdout. Iterable holdout group (blast campaigns). | The "no message" arm for the whole programme, which is exactly what our calibration failure needed (Hillstrom 10x overestimate was fixed by a randomised control). At 10M users, even 1% is 100,000 control users, enough to train and test an uplift model. | It measures programme-level lift, not each message or offer. It is only as good as the discipline around it: Braze cannot edit it (disable and recreate), Braze excludes API campaigns and exclusion-labelled messages, CleverTap controls do not apply to API campaigns. **If our sends go through API-triggered campaigns, the platform's global control may not cover them (verify).** Control membership must be exportable. |
| **Per-campaign control (send versus no send)** | Braze campaign control group (needs conversion events). Braze Canvas Experiment Paths (up to 4 paths plus a control group). MoEngage campaign control group (removes users ineligible for frequency cap, preference or reachability before the split, so control is drawn from the eligible pool, which is the right comparison). Adobe AJO content experiment and path experiment holdouts (10% default). Customer.io holdout tests. | Per-campaign randomised treatment effect, across thousands of historical campaigns at an enterprise. | Each is a separate test with its own audience. Pooling needs a clean campaign-by-campaign export of assignment. Adobe's content-experiment holdout applies to one action only, after which holdout profiles can receive other actions. |
| **Randomised action choice** | Pega Customer Decision Hub adaptive models: a control group of customers (best practice 2%, or by attribute) gets random propensities, so the action shown is random. New outbound actions are shown to only 2% of the population until the model matures (200 positive responses). Pega Impact Analyzer runs alternative prioritisation and policy experiments against a small control group. Adobe Target Auto-Target and Automated Personalization reserve a random-experience control (50/50 to evaluate, 90/10 for always-on). | Random choice among actions with a known propensity (1 over the number of eligible actions), which is the best input for off-policy evaluation of an action choice. | Interaction History export access and retention (below). Control is a small share, so per-segment effects need volume. |
| **Bandit logs** | BrazeAI Decisioning Studio (OfferFit): Braze describes a randomised controlled trial with a business-as-usual control. A partner overview describes 80% AI, 10% random control and 10% holdout (third-party, typical not default). | Randomised arms plus logged agent choices. | Adaptive allocation means propensities vary over time and must be logged to be usable. Reviews on G2 mention reporting challenges. |
| **Experimentation platforms** | Optimizely Experimentation Events Export (decision events are the assignment records, daily Parquet). Statsig Warehouse Native (assignment source SQL, deduplicated first-exposure rollups, holdouts 1% to 10%). Eppo (warehouse-native, now part of Datadog per a second-hand source). | Randomised assignment logs for product and app experiments, including holdouts that measure cumulative effect. | They hold product experiments (UI, pricing, ranking), not messaging, unless the client routes messaging tests through them. |

**Where this does not give us causal lift.**
- **Salesforce Marketing Cloud:** Path Optimizer is A/B-style testing with automatic winner selection (up to 10 paths per a community post). I found no official statement of a standing control or a holdout in Journey Builder. Third-party guides say a holdout needs a "Run Once" journey **(verify)**. Einstein Send Time Optimization recommends a control group to measure its lift, which implies none is created by default.
- **Airship:** nothing found on holdouts or exports. **Attentive, Postscript, Klaviyo and Mailchimp:** see the small-shop section.
- Opens remain contaminated by Apple Mail Privacy Protection (Apple clients were about 62% of tracked opens in July 2026 per a Litmus-citing blog, single source). Use clicks, app actions and orders as outcomes.

**Reading for Autonomy.** For an enterprise client the first job is not to build a holdout but to **find and export the one they already run**: the platform's global control, the per-campaign controls, Pega's random control, and the experimentation platform's assignment table. Then (a) calibrate expected value per message against randomised outcomes, (b) train an uplift model on treated versus control (MegaFon and Lenta in our tests show a T-learner earns what our agent could not), and (c) keep our own seeded holdout for the decisions we make, because the platform control only covers sends it controls.

### 2. How much per-person depth is there compared with purchases?

Much more than at small shops, and it varies by vertical.

| Vertical | Purchase or core outcome frequency per person | Other per-person signals available from the engagement tool | Per-person learning feasible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food delivery and consumer apps | Swiggy reported 4.01 orders per monthly transacting user in Q4 FY26 (down from 4.22) and 4.07 for FY26, on 25.2M MTUs. Zomato (Eternal) reported 24.9M MTUs in the Dec 2025 quarter, no frequency figure found. So about 48 orders per active user per year, against about 1 for the Shopify datasets we tested. | App sessions, screen and product events, push, in-app and email sends, opens, clicks and conversions streamed from Braze Currents or equivalents. Heavy use of in-app and push (higher volume than email). | **Yes for active users.** Dozens of outcomes per person per year. Still thin for the long tail of users who order once or twice. |
| SaaS | Activation and upgrade events: a few per user lifetime; usage events are many | Product usage events (often in a CDP or warehouse, not the messaging tool), email and in-app engagement | Per-person on usage depth, pooled for conversion |
| Banks, insurers | Product take-up or renewal about 1 a year; card transactions are frequent but not the marketing outcome | Contact history, offer responses (Pega Interaction History), channel preferences, consent | Pooled models and uplift, not per-person. Rare outcomes, long delays. |
| Telecoms | Monthly bill and top-up cycle; churn is the rare outcome | Usage, top-ups, offer responses, contact history | Pooled models, plus cycle timing per person |
| Large retailers | Purchase frequency is low per person but loyalty-card data is deeper | Loyalty events, app and web events | Mixed |

Engagement exposure per person is also higher: enterprise programmes commonly run email, push, in-app, SMS and content cards at once. The per-day volume is not published in what I found, so I make no claim on it. Frequency capping rules in the tool (MoEngage filters frequency-capped users before assigning control, Klaviyo-style smart sending skips) shape what is logged.

**What it means:** per-person bandit learning is credible for high-frequency apps and still not for insurers and most banks, where a pooled uplift model on randomised control data is the right tool. This matches the existing finding that per-person learning needs outcome frequency we only get in some verticals.

### 3. Who already does next-best-action, and where is the gap?

| Vendor | What it decides today | Basis | Complement or compete |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pega Customer Decision Hub** | Next-best-action across channels in banks and telcos: adaptive (Bayesian) models per action learn from responses, arbitration by propensity times value with business rules and engagement policies, a 2% random control group, Impact Analyzer experiments, a one-to-one business case per action | Leader in regulated sectors. Heavy, services-led implementations. | Compete at the top end. The gap is in measured calibration: Pega scores propensity, and its value-versus-no-contact logic depends on how the client sets levers. We could complement as an independent incrementality and calibration check, reading Interaction History. |
| **BrazeAI Decisioning Studio (OfferFit)** | Per-customer choice of channel, message, offer, timing and frequency with contextual bandits, replacing A/B testing, with a randomised control | Sits on Braze (also standalone, reads from CDPs or warehouses). **Where a gap appears (my analysis, not a vendor claim):**
1. **Silos.** Each tool optimises its own channel or content inside its own estate (Braze for app, Adobe or SFMC for email, Pega for bank offers). Large clients run several. A vendor-neutral layer can decide across them, and across goals, with one contact budget and one fatigue model.
2. **The "whether and how much" question.** Ranking and bandit tools choose among messages. Few tell a client when not contacting a person has higher value, and the evidence for that sits in the randomised controls described above. Our own calibration failure (10x overstated value per message) shows how easily this goes wrong, and it is what an independent incrementality-calibrated layer would address.
3. **Independent measurement.** Vendors grade their own homework. A client with a platform-wide control group and exports can have us re-estimate lift outside the vendor's reporting. Reviews of Decisioning Studio mention reporting difficulty, which is a hint, not proof.
4. **Regulated sectors.** Pega's strength means a bank or telco that already runs it is the hardest sale. Insurers and telcos without it, and digital-native consumer apps that build in-house, are more open. I found no public evidence of what Zomato, Swiggy or Uber Eats actually use.

**Integration modes, in order of preference for a big client**
1. **Warehouse-native read.** Read from Snowflake or BigQuery where Braze, Iterable, Pega or Optimizely already land data. This is how large clients prefer it (no vendor copy of person-level data).
2. **Score write-back.** Write an uplift or send-or-not score into the profile or a decision table that their journey reads. Fits the "slow model uplift score" fix already planned.
3. **API-triggered send.** Our decision fires their campaign (connector note). Check that their global control covers API sends.
4. **Decision API.** Their journey calls us (Braze Connected Content, Pega external model call). Highest value and highest integration burden.

## Access caveats in one place

- Braze: Currents and Snowflake Data Sharing are paid entitlements. Global control excludes API campaigns and exclusion-labelled messages. Global control cannot be edited. Backfill unconfirmed. Salesforce acquisition is announced, not closed.
- MoEngage: global control API works only for uploaded-list groups, not random-allocation groups.
- CleverTap: system control group does not cover API campaigns. Snowflake export in private beta.
- WebEngage: control groups are off until the account team enables them.
- Iterable: Snowflake share lags up to 12 hours, holdout on blast campaigns only.
- Adobe: journey-level holdout is limited availability, and holdout is scoped per action in content experiments.
- Pega: IH files are kept 180 days, so copy them out. Export defaults vary by release.
- Optimizely: S3 delivery ends 30 Nov 2026, move to GCS. BigQuery needs a support request or higher plan.
- Everywhere: opens are contaminated by Apple MPP. Use clicks, app actions and orders.

## Small shops (earlier scope, short)

- **Klaviyo:** events API gives Placed Order, Viewed Product, Clicked Email and the rest, history depth unconfirmed (aggregate queries reach back at most 5 years). Predictive fields (`historic_clv`, `predicted_clv`, `total_clv`, `churn_probability`, `expected_date_of_next_order`, `average_days_between_orders`, `predicted_number_of_orders`) only for accounts with at least 500 buyers, 180 days of order history and recent orders, and only as current values, so snapshot them daily. Global holdout needs 400,000 total profiles (about 5% recommended, immutable, about 3 months). Campaign A/B reporting bundles variants in the Reporting API, so use raw events. A/B tests are variant versus variant, not send versus no send. Klaviyo 2026 benchmarks: flows are 5.3% of sends and about 41% of email revenue, clicks 5.58% (flows) versus 1.69% (campaigns).
- **Mailchimp:** member activity returns only the last 50 events, no open or click webhooks. **HubSpot:** marketing email events are separate from the Events API (which has page views). **Attentive, Postscript:** forward-only webhooks, no confirmed history read.
- Per-person depth there: about one purchase per customer, email clicks roughly 2 a year (derived), so pooled uplift only.
